# SECTION 2: Thames Path Research

## 2a. Teddington to Richmond

**Distance:** Approximately 5 km / 3 miles one-way.
**Walking time:** Around 1 hour 45 minutes at a relaxed pace.
**Direction:** Can be walked in either direction; Teddington → Richmond follows the south bank.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Teddington: Cedar Road car park (postcode TW11 9AN, 22 bays, Mon–Sat 8am–6:30pm, chargeable); Teddington Pool car park (TW11 8EZ, 42 bays, Mon–Fri 8:30am–6:30pm, Sat 8:30am–5pm, chargeable). Free parking also at Bushy Park (short walk).
- Richmond: Mostly resident-only parking zones; public transport strongly preferred. Richmond Station (TW9 2NA) is the practical option for a one-way walk.

**Route Character:** Teddington Lock (the tidal limit of the Thames) → Petersham Meadows → Richmond Hill → Richmond riverside. Passes historic houses, water meadows, and the iconic Petersham Meadows where cattle graze seasonally. The path is well-surfaced and flat throughout.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Good in meadow sections; leads required near cattle (Petersham Meadows has seasonal grazing).
- Water access: Thames accessible at multiple points along the south bank; dogs can paddle safely in calm sections.
- The stretch is popular and well-trafficked, especially at weekends; weekday visits recommended for more space.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Lass O'Richmond Hill** — explicitly described as "dog-loving," gives treats and provides water bowls.
- **The White Hart** — riverside, dog-welcoming.
- **The Waterman's Arms** — dogs welcome on lead in the downstairs bar area.
- **The Mute Swan** (near Hampton Court, slightly further) — water and treats provided, hosts monthly dog walks.

**Facilities:** Multiple cafes, toilets, and a full range of services at Richmond town centre. Dog waste bins present throughout the Richmond/Teddington corridor. This is a well-maintained urban-fringe section.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Combination of tidal Thames drama at Teddington Lock, the rural quiet of Petersham Meadows, and the Georgian charm of Richmond riverside — all within SW London. Ideal as a year-round half-day walk with return by train.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** Dogs on leads near cattle in Petersham Meadows (typically spring/summer grazing). No formal CRoW Act open access restrictions — this is a public right of way throughout.

## 2b. Shepperton to Walton-on-Thames

**Distance:** Approximately 3–4 km / 2–2.5 miles of direct riverside path; the route can be extended by looping via Weybridge and the River Wey confluence, making a longer circular of 5–7 miles.
**Walking time:** 45 minutes to 1.5 hours linear; 2–3 hours circular.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Shepperton: Public car park on the Las Palmas Estate, Shepperton (TW17 area). Also parking at Walton Bridge car park (large, postcode KT12 area).
- Note: A ferry crossing (the Shepperton Ferry) links the two banks at this point and has a small charge; it involves steps and is seasonal/tide-dependent. The walk is more manageable as an out-and-back from one bank or by using Walton Bridge.

**Route Character:** Level surfaced path from Walton Bridge upstream to Shepperton Lock. Open riverside with views across to Desborough Island. The path passes D'Oyly Carte Island, Walton Riverbank, and approaches the mouth of the River Wey Navigation. Gentle and relatively quiet by Surrey Thames standards.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Moderate — good open stretches on the Walton and Shepperton banks; leads advisable near the ferry landing and busy lock areas.
- Water access: River accessible at multiple points; relatively calm water here upstream of the tidal section.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Thames Court** (Shepperton, Vintage Inn) — confirmed dog-friendly, provides water and treats, has outdoor terrace. Dogs allowed in bar area.
- **The Anglers** (Walton-on-Thames, KT12 2PF) — Thames-side outdoor seating, dog welcome, riverside park access adjacent.

**Facilities:** Limited in comparison to more visited sections. Carry waste bags; bin provision patchy on this rural Surrey stretch. Parking available at both ends but modest in size.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Quieter, more local feel compared to the Richmond/Kingston stretches. The Desborough Island loop adds variety and the River Wey confluence is historically significant. Good for those based in the Surrey commuter belt seeking an uncrowded riverside walk.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** No specific restrictions identified beyond standard leads-near-livestock guidance.

## 2c. Pangbourne to Goring (The Goring Gap)

**Distance:** Approximately 5 miles / 8 km one-way.
**Walking time:** 2–2.5 hours.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Goring: Station car park (RG8 9HH area) or village parking. Goring-on-Thames Station (GWR) gives good public transport options for a linear walk.
- Pangbourne: Station car park or village parking (RG8 7AX area). Pangbourne Station is also on the GWR line to Reading/Oxford.
- The section is frequently walked in both directions using the train between the two stations (about 5 minutes apart by rail), making it an excellent linear day walk.

**Route Character:** Passes through the Goring Gap — one of the most dramatic points on the entire Thames. The river cuts between the Chiltern Hills (east) and the North Wessex Downs (west), both of which are Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The path passes through wildflower meadows and wooded hillsides, with wide views across the valley. Three ancient routes converge here: the Ridgeway, the Icknield Way, and the Thames itself.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: The walk passes through several fields with cattle, so leads are required in those sections. Open meadow sections offer good off-lead stretches.
- The route is noted as largely stile-free (Chilterns National Landscape lists it as such), making it accessible for larger dogs.
- Prone to flooding in wetter conditions; sturdy footwear recommended.
- Note as of late 2025: A temporary diversion is in place through the Parish of Streatley due to path access issues. Check the National Trails website for current status before visiting.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- In **Goring**: The Miller of Mansfield (gastropub, dog-friendly bar and garden) and The Catherine Wheel.
- In **Pangbourne**: The Cross Keys (riverside, dog-friendly garden).
- Both villages are charming and offer post-walk refreshments.

**Facilities:** Toilets and parking at both villages. Cafes available in both Goring and Pangbourne. Dog bins at village approaches but sparse mid-route — carry your own bags.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Considered one of the most scenically outstanding stretches of the entire 184-mile Thames Path. The dual AONB landscape, historic chalk geography, and Goring Gap drama make this a genuinely special walk. Not well known to a national audience despite the quality, making it a high-value content gap for Sniffout.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** March–July: leads near cattle as per standard countryside code. Ground-nesting birds in meadow areas during spring may require keeping to marked paths.

## 2d. Oxford / Port Meadow

**Distance:** The meadow itself is 136.9 hectares. The circuit of Port Meadow from Wolvercote to Binsey and back is approximately 4–5 miles; the wider Thames Path and canal loop is 6–8 miles.
**Walking time:** 1.5–3 hours depending on route taken.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Wolvercote car park (OX2 8PN) — free. Adjacent to the meadow's northern end with toilets and dog waste bins.
- Godstow Road entrance also has a picnic area. Central Oxford access via Walton Well Road (Jericho end).
- Port Meadow is walkable from Oxford city centre on foot (~25 minutes from the train station).

**Route Character:** Port Meadow is an ancient common — one of the oldest unploughed land in England — that borders the Thames (and the Oxford Canal is close by). The River Thames runs along the western edge. Burgess Field Nature Reserve (35 ha) adjoins the meadow to the north. The landscape is wide, flat, and genuinely expansive — rare in urban areas. The path can combine with the Thames towpath to reach Godstow Lock and the historic Trout Inn.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Dogs may be off-lead throughout the meadow EXCEPT near livestock (cattle and horses graze year-round on the common) and during bird nesting season (leads required, approximately March–July).
- The river is accessible for swimming; the meadow floods regularly in winter, creating shallow temporary lakes that dogs love.
- Note: The meadow can be very muddy November through March.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Trout Inn** (Lower Wolvercote, OX2 8PN) — historic riverside inn at Godstow Lock with large riverside garden and terrace. Confirmed dog-friendly. Free parking. Arguably one of the best-known dog walk pubs in Oxfordshire.
- **The Perch** (Binsey, OX2 0NG) — 17th-century thatched pub on the west side of the meadow. Large garden, welcomes muddy dogs explicitly. Parking available outside.

**Facilities:** Free parking at Wolvercote car park. Toilets and dog waste bins at the Godstow Road entrance. Static picnic benches and BBQs available. No cafe within the meadow itself but extensive amenities at both The Trout and The Perch.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Ancient common with extraordinary historical weight — grazed since Saxon times. The combination of complete openness (rare in England), iconic pubs, proximity to Oxford city centre, and year-round access makes it a top-tier urban dog walk destination. Dogs swimming in the flooded winter meadow is a visual and experience highlight.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** Leads near livestock year-round; leads near ground-nesting birds March–July. Check flooding status before visiting in winter.

## 2e. Henley to Marlow

**Distance:** 9 miles / 14.5 km one-way.
**Walking time:** 3–4 hours.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Henley: Henley Bridge (RG9 1BH). Henley has several public car parks; Hart Street car park (RG9 2AR) is central. Henley-on-Thames Station (GWR) is well-served.
- Marlow: Higginson Park, Marlow (SL7 2AZ area). Marlow Station (Elizabeth Line / GWR) is close to the start of the walk.
- Return: Arriva buses 328/329 run every 30 minutes between Henley and Marlow. Alternatively, arrange two cars or use trains via Reading.

**Route Character:** Walking downstream from Henley past the famous regatta course, rowing clubs, and Remenham Hill (wooded slopes of the Chilterns), through Hambledon Lock (one of the prettiest on the Thames), past the converted Hambleden Mill on its river island, through Hurley and Bisham to Marlow. The terrain is almost entirely flat. The Chiltern Hills provide a scenic backdrop for most of the route.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Good; the route passes through open river meadows for extended stretches. Leads required near the occasional livestock field and at lock areas.
- River access is available throughout for dogs who enjoy swimming; the Thames here is generally calm.
- The Flower Pot Hotel at Aston (10–15 minute detour off the path) and The Baskerville Arms at Shiplake are excellent mid-route dog stops.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Baskerville Arms** (Shiplake, RG9 3NU) — outdoor dog shower, dog food and snacks sold, dog-friendly rooms, described as possibly the most dog-friendly pub in Henley area. Large garden.
- **The Flower Pot Hotel** (Aston, RG9 3DG) — 10–15 minute detour off path from Hambledon Lock; popular walking pub, dog-friendly garden.
- **The Two Brewers** (Marlow, SL7 1NQ) — traditional pub, dog-friendly, on the route in St Peter Street, established 1727.
- **The Chequers** (Marlow, SL7 1LW) — Brakspear's pub, dog-friendly. In Henley: Angel on the Bridge, The Bull, The Anchor, The Little Angel (all Brakspear's pubs with dog-welcome policy).

**Facilities:** Toilets in both Henley and Marlow town centres. Henley has a full range of cafes, restaurants. Marlow riverside has Higginson Park with good facilities. Dog bins at main entry/exit points.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Classic Thames Valley countryside walk combining Henley's regatta glamour with deeply rural mid-river scenery. Hambleden Lock is widely regarded as one of the most picturesque on the river. The Chiltern Hills backdrop throughout gives genuine landscape quality. Exceptional pub density for a single walking day.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** Standard leads-near-livestock where fields adjoin the path. No specific seasonal restrictions identified beyond those.

## 2f. Chiswick to Kew (Strand on the Green)

**Distance:** Approximately 2.5–3 miles one-way from Chiswick Station to Kew Bridge / Kew Gardens. Can be extended to Kew Gardens Station or Richmond.
**Walking time:** 45 minutes to 1.5 hours.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Chiswick: Chiswick Railway Station (W4 5QL) — 15-minute walk down Chiswick Lane to reach the towpath at Strand on the Green. Limited street parking (W4 area, resident zones apply weekdays).
- Kew: Kew Bridge (TW8 0EN) or Kew Gardens Station (TW9 3JX). The Kew area has some public car parks off Kew Green and on Station Parade.

**Route Character:** The Strand on the Green section (~500 metres) is a historic waterside terrace of Georgian and Victorian houses immediately downriver from Kew Bridge. The Thames Path continues between Chiswick and the gardens. Oliver's Island sits mid-river adjacent to The Bulls Head pub — a Civil War landmark. The path passes boathouses, elegant riverside architecture, and connects to Kew Green and the Royal Botanic Gardens (dogs on leads in the Gardens but welcome).

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Limited — this is an urban/suburban section and the towpath is narrow and busy, particularly at weekends. Dogs should generally be on leads.
- River access: The tidal Thames here is not safe for dog swimming (strong currents, steep banks in many areas). Not a paddling stretch.
- Quieter on weekday mornings.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Bulls Head** (Strand on the Green, W4 3PQ) — riverside inn, recently refurbished, outdoor seating with river views, dog-friendly.
- **The Bell and Crown** (Strand on the Green, W4 3PQ) — 18th-century pub, conservatory, dog-friendly.
- **The Steam Packet** (adjacent to Kew Bridge) — dog-friendly, river views.
- **The Lamb** (Chiswick area) — spacious pub with outdoor patio, confirmed dog-friendly with treats and water brought to dogs.

**Facilities:** Full urban amenities throughout. Kew Gardens café (dogs not inside the café building). Public toilets at Kew Bridge. Good transport connections (District Line, Overground).

**What Makes It Distinctive:** The prettiest short riverside walk in West London. Strand on the Green's historic terrace is a genuine hidden gem for those who don't know it. The connection to Kew's botanic gardens makes this an outstanding half-day destination. Content differentiation: this section rarely appears on dog-specific walking sites despite its quality.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** No specific seasonal restrictions on the towpath. Leads only in Kew Gardens if entering.

## 2g. Cookham to Marlow

**Distance:** 8.6 miles / 13.8 km one-way. Often walked as a linear with car or bus return.
**Walking time:** 3.5–4 hours.

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Cookham: National Trust car park at Cookham Moor (grid ref SU892853, postcode SL6 9SG). £2 parking for non-NT members (free for NT members). This is adjacent to the Thames towpath start.
- Marlow: Higginson Park car park (SL7 2AZ) or Marlow Station.
- Return: Bus services connect Marlow and Cookham/Maidenhead.

**Route Character:** From Cookham Moor the path follows the Thames past Cock Marsh (NT open access land — a SSSI for lowland wetland), Bourne End, Hedsor Water, and into Marlow. Cock Marsh is one of the finest unimproved flood meadows in the Chilterns, purchased by local residents in 1934 and donated to the NT. The section passes through quiet water meadows, with wooded Chiltern slopes on the Bucks bank.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Cock Marsh and the open meadow sections offer excellent off-lead walking. Leads required near any cattle grazing and near the busy stretches near Marlow lock and town.
- River access: Multiple points where dogs can access the Thames.
- The Bounty pub is boat/foot only access (no road), giving the stretch a genuinely remote feel for a home counties walk.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Bounty** (Cockmarsh, accessible only by river or foot, SL6 0JG area) — one of the most celebrated dog-friendly riverside pubs in the South East. Doggy menu, dog chews available for a charity donation, dog water bowls, outdoor riverside seating. Boat access or walk-in only.
- **The White Oak** (Cookham, SL6 9SH) — Michelin-rated contemporary gastropub in Cookham village centre. Large garden and terrace. Dogs very welcome in the bar.
- **The Two Brewers** (Marlow, SL7 1NQ) — as above, traditional, dog-friendly endpoint pub.

**Facilities:** NT car park at Cookham Moor, toilets in Cookham village and Marlow. Cookham village has a cafe and shops. The Bounty has no road access but has its own facilities.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** The Bounty pub alone is worth the walk — one of the most unusual and dog-welcoming riverside pubs in England. National Trust land at Cock Marsh adds conservation value and open-access legitimacy. A genuinely distinctive Sniffout page opportunity: "The Walk to the Pub You Can Only Reach on Foot."

**Seasonal Restrictions:** Leads near cattle at Cock Marsh (seasonal grazing). Ground-nesting birds (March–July): keep to paths in SSSI wetland sections.

## 2h. Lechlade to Kelmscott

**Distance:** 8.6 miles / 13.8 km circular from Lechlade; the linear Lechlade–Kelmscott stretch is approximately 4–5 miles.
**Walking time:** 3.5 hours (circular).

**Start/End and Parking:**
- Lechlade: Car park by St Lawrence's Church, Lechlade (GL7 3AD). 2 hours free; additional parking in the town. Paid parking £3.20/6 hours via app. Limited public transport to Lechlade — car or bike recommended.
- Kelmscott: Very small village. Limited roadside parking at Kelmscott Manor entrance (NT property, William Morris's home) — SN7 8SE.

**Route Character:** The upper Thames at Lechlade and Kelmscott is among the most remote and peaceful stretches of the entire Thames Path. Almost no boat traffic above Lechlade, very few walkers. Wide grassy riverbanks, ancient willows, flat meadow landscape. Kelmscott Manor (William Morris's home) is a landmark — open to visitors at certain times. The circular walk crosses from one bank to the other via Halfpenny Bridge (the last bridge before the river becomes unnavigable for most boats).

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Excellent. The flat meadows on both banks are outstanding off-lead territory. Very few other walkers in the area compared to lower Thames sections.
- River access: Dogs can swim freely in the upper Thames here; the water is clear, relatively shallow, and calm.
- Can become very muddy October–March.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Plough Inn** (Kelmscott, GL7 3HG) — traditional Cotswold inn at the halfway point of the circular walk. Dogs amble through the bar according to multiple walkers' accounts. Good selection of ales and food. Beer garden. Confirmed dog-friendly.
- In Lechlade: **The New Inn** and **The Riverside** (with terrace overlooking the river) — both reported as dog-friendly in visitor accounts.

**Facilities:** Toilets and limited parking in Lechlade. Post office/shop in Lechlade. Very few facilities mid-route — self-sufficient preparation essential. No cafes between Lechlade and Kelmscott on the route itself.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** The most remote and peaceful day section of the Thames Path near civilisation. William Morris associations (Kelmscott Manor) add cultural depth. The combination of complete solitude, exceptional off-lead territory, and a quintessential Cotswold pub at the halfway mark makes this a high-quality but underexposed Sniffout page.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** Standard riverside livestock guidelines. The path may be impassable after prolonged flooding in winter. CRoW Act open access provisions: leads required near livestock and 1 March–31 July on open access land near the river.

# SECTION 3: Canal and Waterway Research

## 3a. Regent's Canal, London

**Overview:** The Regent's Canal runs 9 miles / 14.5 km from Little Venice (Paddington) to Limehouse Basin, passing through Regent's Park, Camden, King's Cross, Islington (with a short tunnel section), Hackney, and Victoria Park. The towpath is continuous (one short tunnel detour at Islington via street).

**Best Sections for Dogs:**
- **Little Venice to Camden** (2.5 miles): Prettiest opening section. Wide towpath, colourful narrowboats, Regent's Park alongside. Comfortable off-lead for well-trained dogs.
- **Camden to King's Cross** (2 miles): Busier, markets and cafés, still pleasant. The Narrowboat pub is on this stretch.
- **Victoria Park section** (east London): The towpath alongside Victoria Park (dogs off-lead in the park) is the best off-lead opportunity. Less urban feel than Camden end.
- **Avoiding peak weekend times** strongly recommended for any section — very busy Saturday/Sunday afternoons.

**Off-Lead Rules:** Canal & River Trust policy does not mandate leads on the towpath, but local conditions (cyclists, other dogs, wildfowl) mean most responsible owners keep dogs on leads. Victoria Park sections and early mornings offer the most comfortable off-lead experience. Definitely leads near locks.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Narrowboat** (Islington, N1 7RJ) — directly on the canal towpath. Staff provide doggie water bowl, biscuits, and a blanket in cold weather. One of London's most celebrated canal dog pubs.
- **The Engineer** (Primrose Hill/Camden, NW1 8SR) — dog-welcoming, good food.
- **The Barge House** (Hoxton) — canalside, dog-friendly.

**Facilities:** Urban amenities throughout. Dog waste bins at main access points. Canal towpath is flat, well-maintained, and largely paved in the inner London sections — good for all-weather walking.

**Best Start Points:** Little Venice (W2 6NE, Warwick Avenue tube); Camden Lock (NW1 8AF, Camden Town tube); Victoria Park (E9 7DE, Cambridge Heath or Hackney Wick bus/Overground).

**What Makes It Distinctive:** The quintessential London dog walk — utterly flat, immensely varied, with London Zoo, floating markets, and world-class street food all accessible from the towpath. A linear walk that can be done in sections using the tube for return. High search volume potential.

## 3b. Grand Union Canal — Best Sections Outside London

**Leicester Arm / Foxton Locks:**
- The Leicester arm (Grand Union Canal Leicester Line) passes through Foxton Locks — a famous flight of 10 staircase locks with an adjacent inclined plane museum. Dogs should be on leads around the lock flight itself (safety, boat traffic), but excellent off-lead meadow walking extends in both directions from the locks.
- Dog waste bins confirmed at lock numbers 26, 36, 41, 43, and 45.
- Pubs at Foxton: The Bridge Inn and The Black Horse are both dog-friendly. The café on site at the lock museum has a dog-friendly outdoor area.
- Start: Foxton Locks car park (LE16 7RA).

**Northampton Arm:**
- Connects the main line to the River Nene. Linear towpath through gentle Northamptonshire countryside. Quiet and relatively undiscovered. Limited specific dog pub information found — confidence: medium.

**Warwick/Leamington Section:**
- **Hatton Locks** ("Stairway to Heaven") — 21 locks over 3.2 km is the highlight. 5-mile circular walk from Hatton, with excellent views and changing scenery. Hatton Country World nearby (dog-friendly).
- The **Leamington Spa to Warwick** stretch follows the canal and River Leam through Leam Valley Local Nature Reserve. 6+ miles, dog-friendly, good for combining canal and riverside.
- Note: Towpath sections here are leads-only by convention due to cyclists and boat traffic. Alternatives involve taking field paths off the towpath.
- Start: Hatton (CV35 7JL area); Leamington Spa town centre.

## 3c. Kennet and Avon Canal

**Overview:** 87-mile canal from Reading to Bath. The most scenically outstanding and dog-friendly sections are in the western half, from Devizes westward.

**Bath to Bradford-on-Avon (9 miles / approximately 14.5 km):**
- Flattest and most accessible section. Paved towpath throughout, suitable for all weathers. Passes Bathampton, Limpley Stoke Valley, and the Avoncliff and Dundas Aqueducts.
- **The George Inn** (Bathampton, BA2 6EL) — just off the canal at Bathampton, dog-friendly, historic village pub.
- **The Lock Inn** (Bradford-on-Avon, BA15 1LG) — directly on the canal at Bradford Lock. Confirmed dog-friendly indoors and out. Excellent food, popular with cyclists and walkers.
- **The Barge Inn** (Bradford-on-Avon area, not to be confused with Honeystreet branch) — canalside, dog-friendly.

**Bradford-on-Avon to Devizes (via Caen Hill locks) (~15 miles):**
- The Caen Hill flight (29 locks in 2 miles) is one of the most dramatic engineering spectacles in British canal history. The broad reservoir pound at each lock gives dogs space to explore.
- **The Barge Inn** (Seend Cleeve, SN12 6PH) — right on the canal. Dogs greeted with treats on arrival, special dog water barrel outside, outdoor garden. One of the best canal dog pubs in England. Confirmed dog-friendly throughout.

**Devizes to Hungerford (~28 miles, best walked in day sections):**
- Crofton Pumping Station and Crofton Beam Engines (SN8 3DW) — world's oldest working steam engines in original location. Family-friendly visit mid-walk.
- **The Dundas Arms** (Kintbury, RG17 9UT) — right on the canal and railway station. Dogs and muddy boots explicitly welcomed. Outstanding position at Kintbury Wharf.
- **The Barge Inn** (Honeystreet, SN9 5PS) — legendary quirky pub on the canal with Crop Circle Museum association, outdoor canalside seating, views to Marlborough Downs. Confirmed dog-friendly.
- **The Hourglass** (Devizes, SN10 area) — terrace garden overlooking the water.

**Seasonal Restrictions:** Canal & River Trust seasonal stoppages for maintenance (typically winter); check canalplan.uk for current notices. No specific dog access seasonal restrictions identified beyond standard towpath guidance (leads near boat mooring areas, duck/cygnet nesting areas in spring).

## 3d. Oxford Canal — Cropredy and Banbury to Fenny Compton

**Overview:** The Oxford Canal runs from Coventry to Oxford, 77 miles. The southern section from Banbury through Cropredy and north toward Fenny Compton is the most dog-friendly stretch.

**Banbury to Cropredy (6 miles one-way):**
- Starts at Banbury Station (OX16 5AA), through Bridge Street Park, Spiceball Park, Grimsbury Woodland Nature Reserve, and Roman Meadow Banbury Country Park, before reaching the open countryside canal towpath to Cropredy.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs in Cropredy (OX17 1PG):**
- **The Red Lion** — one of the village's two dog-friendly pubs.

**Off-lead Suitability:** Good in the open countryside sections between Banbury and Cropredy. The towpath is quiet compared to southern canals.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Cropredy is famous for its annual Fairport Convention folk festival (August) and the Battle of Cropredy Bridge (English Civil War, 1644). The walk combines industrial canal heritage with quintessential English village character. The Banbury to Fenny Compton extension is even quieter and more rural.

## 3e. Shropshire Union Canal

**Chester to Ellesmere (approximately 20 miles, best done as day sections):**

**Chester Section (city to Bunbury, ~14 miles):**
- Chester is entered via the Northgate Locks. The elevated embankment section leaving Chester gives elevated views over the surrounding countryside — one of the most dramatic canal features in England.
- The towpath through Chester connects to the city's medieval walls, Roman ruins, and rows architecture — unusual combination of canal and historic city walking.

**Ellesmere Section:**
- The canal passes close to The Mere at Ellesmere (the largest of Shropshire's glacial lakes, 52 hectares) and Colemere Country Park.
- **The Red Lion** (Ellesmere, SY12 area) — close to the canal, dog-friendly.
- Shropshire's Lake District Circular walk (7.5 miles, 316 AllTrails reviews) takes in The Mere, Colemere, Blake Mere, and the Llangollen Canal towpath from Ellesmere.
- The Meres Meander walks (4 circular routes) start from Ellesmere town centre and combine the canal with the meres, passing Blakemere and Colemere.

**Market Drayton Section:**
- Market Drayton Picnic Area is adjacent to the canal. The town is known as "the home of gingerbread." Relatively quiet stretch.

**Off-Lead Suitability:** Excellent throughout the rural Shropshire sections. Leads near lock flights and boat mooring areas. The Ellesmere meres walks in Colemere Country Park allow off-lead in designated areas.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** The combination of the elevated Northgate Embankment, the Shropshire meres landscape (rare in England — glacial lakes), and the rural isolation of the mid-section make this one of the most varied canal walking experiences in England. Underrepresented on dog walking sites.

## 3f. River Wey Navigation, Surrey (Guildford to Godalming)

**Distance:** 5.5 miles / 9 km one-way; full navigation is 20 miles from the Thames at Weybridge to Godalming.
**Walking time:** 2–2.5 hours one-way (Guildford to Godalming).

**Managed by:** National Trust. The River Wey Navigation (1653) was England's first commercial navigation canal and is now a National Trust property. The towpath runs 20 miles.

**Dog Policy (National Trust):**
- Dogs welcome on leads throughout the towpath; leads specifically required in lock areas and around the tea room/visitor garden at Dapdune Wharf (Guildford).
- Dogs not permitted on the NT boat trips.
- 20 miles of towpath available; rated two pawprints by NT.
- Dog waste bins confirmed at regular intervals (National Trust managed).

**Best Sections:**
- **Dapdune Wharf (Guildford, GU1 4RR) to Cartbridge Wharf**: The NT-recommended day walk. Passes meadows, traditional lock cottages, and historic wharves.
- **Guildford to Godalming full section**: Through Shalford, Broadford Bridge, and Farncombe. The path is wide and well-maintained throughout with excellent river access.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs:**
- **The Row Barge** (Guildford, GU1 area) — at the start of the route, bar area and garden dog-friendly, treats often available.
- **The Seahorse** (Shalford, GU4 8BU) — en route, fine menu, attractive outdoor space.
- **The Ragged Robin** (Godalming, GU7 area) — gastro pub 6.3 km into the walk, garden overlooking the river, water and treats complimentary for dogs. Confirmed dog-friendly.

**Facilities:** Dapdune Wharf visitor centre (Guildford) has toilets, dog waste bins, information. Godalming has full town centre amenities. The towpath is surfaced throughout and passable year-round.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** National Trust ownership ensures excellent maintenance and facilities. Historically England's first commercial canal. Highly relevant to Sniffout as it already covers Surrey heavily — this adds a premium linear walk to the Surrey content offering. Good rail connections at both ends (Guildford Station, Godalming Station).

**Seasonal Restrictions:** No dog-specific seasonal restrictions identified. Leads in lock areas and near tea room garden year-round.

## 3g. Wandle Trail (Croydon to Wandsworth)

**Distance:** 12.5 miles / 20 km (full route, Croydon to Wandsworth). Can be sectioned: Carshalton Ponds to Wandsworth is approximately 9 miles.
**Walking time:** 4–5 hours full route.

**Start Points:**
- **Wandle Park, Croydon** (CR0 area) — full 12.5-mile option.
- **Carshalton Ponds** (SM5 3PZ) — popular 9-mile option; better dog walk starting point with pleasant village setting and parking.
- **Morden Hall Park** (SM4 5JD, National Trust) — mid-route entry point; free NT car park available; this is arguably the best section.
- End: **Wandsworth Town** (SW18, near the Thames confluence).

**Route Character:** The River Wandle flows north from its source in Croydon through South London, past Waddon, Mitcham, Morden, Colliers Wood, and Earlsfield to the Thames. The trail passes: King George's Park, Wandle Meadow Nature Park, Morden Hall Park (NT — rose garden, wetland habitats, dog-friendly), Watermeads Nature Reserve, Wilderness Island, and Beddington Park.

**Dog-Specific Info:**
- Off-lead suitability: Dogs can be off-lead in Morden Hall Park (check current NT guidance), Wandle Meadow, and some open sections. Leads required on busy urban stretches.
- Water access: The Wandle is a chalk stream (one of the last in London), fast-flowing, shallow, and rocky-bottomed — ideal for dog paddling. Best paddle spots: Wandle Park (Croydon), Morden Hall Park, Watermeads.
- Carshalton Ponds at the start are attractive but dogs should not swim in the managed ornamental ponds.

**Dog Facilities:** The trail passes through multiple London boroughs (Croydon, Sutton, Merton, Wandsworth). Dog waste bin provision is variable — carry bags. No confirmed specific dog-friendly pub information found along the trail's less urban sections; best pub options are in Colliers Wood or Wandsworth at the endpoints.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** One of the most underrated long urban walks in London. The Wandle is a genuine chalk stream — rare quality for an urban river — and the combination of National Trust Morden Hall Park, wildlife habitats, and industrial heritage (the river powered mills) makes this a richer walk than its urban setting suggests. Strongly differentiated from Sniffout's current Surrey/NT estate content.

## 3h. Lee Valley Park

**Overview:** Lee Valley Regional Park stretches 26 miles from the Thames at East India Docks (London) to Ware in Hertfordshire, covering 10,000 acres. Best managed for dogs at the Hertfordshire/Essex end.

**Best Dog Sections:**
- **River Lee Country Park / Fishers Green** (EN9 2EF area): A hub within the park with wetland habitats, sculptures trail, and wildlife including lapwings and ducks. Good for leads-on wetland walking with wildlife interest. Free car park at Fishers Green closes 6pm; toilets available.
- **Cornmill Meadows** (EN9 1TD): Free car park, adjacent to Fishers Green. The Dragonfly Trail here is a specific dog walk route through wildflower meadows alongside the River Lee.
- **Hertford area / Hertford Heath**: Rural northern end of the park, very quiet, good off-lead meadow walking.

**Important Restriction:**
- **Walthamstow Wetlands** (E17 area): NO dogs permitted — site is a nature reserve and active drinking water reservoir. This is a firm and well-publicised ban. Only trained assistance dogs on duty are allowed. This must be clearly flagged on any Sniffout content about Lee Valley.

**Off-Lead Rules:** Permissive in open meadow areas; leads required near water edges (safety), near livestock, and where signposted. The park broadly applies Countryside Code principles with some additional lead-required zones around sensitive wetlands.

**Dog-Friendly Facilities:** Toilets at Fishers Green and River Lee Country Park. Car parking at multiple points. The park runs its own dog walking licence scheme for commercial dog walkers but not for individual owners.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Vast linear green corridor from London into Hertfordshire, largely unknown to dog walkers outside the immediate area. The wetland bird life (otters have been recorded), river access, and the contrast between the outer urban edge and the rural Hertfordshire end make this a strong content candidate. Significant search volume from Hertfordshire and north-east London.

**Best Routes:**

### River Exe at Topsham

**Character:** Topsham is a historic estuary town on the tidal Exe, with Dutch-influenced merchants' houses, independent shops, and an exceptional dog-friendly culture. Almost all local pubs and cafes welcome dogs.

**Best Routes:**
- **Bowling Green Marsh Circular** from Topsham: A scenic circular combining tidal estuary views, Bowling Green Marsh Nature Reserve, and the Topsham quay. Approximately 3–4 miles.
- **Goat Walk** (Topsham): A short, dramatic elevated path along the estuary edge — one of the most atmospheric short walks in Devon. Dogs off-lead in the adjacent recreation ground and designated dog-friendly field.
- **Exe Estuary Trail** (full, 26 miles): Almost entirely traffic-free, taking in the whole estuary from Dawlish to Exmouth. The Topsham section (accessible from Topsham Station) is one of the finest stretches.

## 4c. Underrated Coastal Walks

### Holkham Beach, Norfolk

**Dog Access:** Dogs welcome all year round. From 1 April to 31 August, a dogs-on-leads policy applies in the most sensitive coastal nesting areas (typically closest to the dune-to-beach transition zone). Over 70% of the beach remains fully accessible for off-lead walking even during the restricted season. Dogs must be on leads through the pinewoods at the Lady Anne's Drive entrance.

**Parking:** Lady Anne's Drive car park, postcode **NR23 1RG**. Pay and display: approximately £9 for a full day (2025 pricing). Free for National Nature Reserve permit holders.

**Facilities:** The Lookout — RIBA Award-winning visitor centre and café at the end of Lady Anne's Drive. Open daily 9:30am–5pm (to 7pm in summer). Toilets accessible, including accessible facilities. Beach wheelchairs available (pre-book, free of charge).

**Distance/Walk Description:** From the car park, a 1-mile walk through the pinewoods on a broad sandy path leads to the beach. The beach itself extends approximately 3 miles to Wells-next-the-Sea to the east and 2 miles toward Burnham Overy to the west. The open sand and dune system provides hours of walking.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** One of the widest beaches in England — the combination of open sky, vast dunes, the ancient pinewoods backdrop, and remarkably low visitor density (despite being well-known) makes this exceptional. The seasonal restrictions are well-managed with clear roping, and the majority of the beach is unrestricted. High-value Sniffout page — Norfolk coast dog walks are high search volume.

---

### Brancaster Beach, Norfolk

**Dog Access:** Dogs allowed all year round. A small dog-free zone exists to the left (east) of the main beach entrance from May to September, but the vast majority of the beach is accessible year-round with dogs.

**Parking:** Postcode **PE31 8AX**. Pay and display car park (National Trust). Note: car park and access road can flood during very high tides — check tidal forecasts before visiting.

**Facilities:** Beach kiosk opposite the car park (ice cream, teas, coffees). Toilets adjacent to car park (open 6am–6pm). NT managed.

**Character:** Wide, flat sandy beach backed by salt marshes and the historic tidal harbour. Quieter and more local-feeling than Holkham despite being only 8 miles west. The dunes and salt marsh extend to Scolt Head Island, a National Nature Reserve (accessible only by boat). Excellent for long flat walks with big sky views.

**Comparison to Holkham:** Brancaster is considerably quieter, more local in feel, has less provision (the Lookout café at Holkham is far superior as a facility base), but has more of a wild/undiscovered character. NT ownership at Brancaster means good maintenance. Both should be covered by Sniffout — they serve different search intents.

### Sandwich Bay, Kent

**Dog Access:** Dogs welcome on the beach year-round. A PSPO (Public Space Protection Order) is in force with a zoned traffic-light system: off-lead under control from the car park to the Prince's enclosure; leads required on specific dune sections around the golf course; exclusion from the mudflats and salt marshes of the Sandwich and Pegwell Bay Nature Reserve (Kent Wildlife Trust).

**Access:** Via the private Sandwich Bay Estate road — a small toll is charged to drive the road (a few pounds; toll gate operates seasonally). Parking available within the estate.

**Facilities:** Small toilet block at the main access area. No café or beach facilities — self-sufficient visits required. The beach is shingle and sand, quiet, and has an "end of the road" feel.

**Character:** Largely shingle beach, very flat, quiet, and backed by one of England's best links golf courses (Royal St George's). The Sandwich and Pegwell Bay NNR to the north has rich wading bird and wildfowl populations. The area is designated Site of Special Scientific Interest.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** The combination of private road access (keeping visitor numbers low), the distinctive shingle/links landscape, and proximity to the historic Cinque Port town of Sandwich makes this unusual. Good for long, flat off-lead walks in a genuinely quiet setting.

### Mersea Island, Essex

**Access:** Via The Strood — a causeway road connecting the island to the mainland. The Strood floods for approximately one week per month during spring tides, for up to an hour at high tide. Plan visits around low-to-mid tide.

**Dog Access:** Dogs welcome at both West Mersea beach and Cudmore Grove Country Park / Beach. Dogs on leads requested during summer months in some sections of the shoreline (specific seasonal dates not confirmed in research — confidence: Medium).

**Parking:** West Mersea is the main village (CO5 8QT). Cudmore Grove Country Park (CO5 8UE) on the eastern tip is a 102-acre country park with parking, picnic areas, and beach access.

**Character:** The island has extraordinary visual variety: salt marshes, mudflats, grassland, and — at Cudmore Grove — a sandy beach that has been likened to the Caribbean. This is England's most easterly inhabited tidal island. The landscape is flat and vast, with huge sky and estuary views. Mersea is famous for its oysters — West Mersea Oyster Bar is a destination in itself.

**Dog-Specific Info:** The full island perimeter walk (approximately 8 miles) uses footpaths and public beaches with planning required to avoid high tide sections. The salt marsh edges can be challenging — boots/paws will get muddy. Cudmore Grove beach is the best quality off-lead beach on the island.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Genuinely unusual — a tidal island, completely flat, with a surreal combination of oyster shacks, salt marsh, and tropical-looking beach. The tidal access dynamic adds an adventure element. Very low coverage on national dog walk sites. High differentiation value for Sniffout.

---

### Dungeness, Kent

**Dog Access:** Dogs welcome on the beach all year round. No seasonal restrictions identified at Dungeness beach itself. The RSPB Dungeness reserve has a dog-friendly discovery trail.

**Parking:** Roadside parking along Dungeness Road (TN29 area) and at the Lighthouse (TN29 9NB). Free, informal.

**Character:** Classified by the Met Office as Britain's only desert. The largest shingle foreland in Europe — flat, treeless, extraordinarily atmospheric. Abandoned fishing huts, two decommissioned lighthouses, Derek Jarman's famous garden (Prospect Cottage), the nuclear power station, sound mirrors (WW2 acoustic detection devices), and driftwood sculptures create a surreal, post-industrial landscape unique in Britain.

**Dog-Specific Info:** The deep shingle is demanding underfoot — suitable for dogs comfortable on loose, shifting surfaces. The beach shelves steeply and the sea has strong currents — swimming is not advisable. Dogs can roam freely across the shingle. The Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Light Railway is dog-friendly and provides an alternative return option.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** There is nowhere else like Dungeness in England. The darkness, the silence, the industrial ruins, and the extraordinary plant communities on the shingle create an experience utterly unlike any conventional dog walk. High photographic value. Excellent content differentiation — currently underserved by dog walk sites.

## 4d. Overlooked Woodland

### Epping Forest

**Size:** 2,400 hectares of ancient forest straddling the Essex/London border from Epping in the north to Wanstead Flats in the south — 7 miles long and up to 2.5 miles wide.

**Management:** City of London Corporation (not Forestry England or the National Trust). The forest has 52 free car parks.

**Off-Lead Rules:** Dogs must be under "effective control" but are NOT required to be on a lead unless they do not respond to commands. Conservation grazing areas (cattle) exist in some sections — leads required around cattle at all times. In practice, the vast majority of the forest is fully accessible off-lead.

**Key Car Parks / Entry Points:**
- **High Beach** (IG10 4AF) — the central hub, with the visitor field centre and tea hut. High Beach car park charges approximately £6 for up to 6 hours.
- **Loughton car park** (IG10 3SZ) — access to Connaught Water loop (7.1 miles, 3 hours, highly rated).
- **Chingford Plain** (E4 area) — access from Queen Elizabeth's Hunting Lodge (dog-friendly grounds).
- Most of the 52 car parks are free.

**Recommended Dog Routes:**
- **Epping Forest Main Path and Green Ride Circular**: 4.5 stars, 2,140 reviews on AllTrails. Considered the best overall route.
- **Connaught Water Loop** (7.1 miles from Loughton): Explores the large ornamental lake and surrounding forest.
- **Ten Forest Waymarked Trails**: Available as one-page maps from the Visitor Centre at High Beach.

**Facilities:** Visitor Centre at High Beach. Tea hut at Pillow Mounds. Boat hire at Hollow Pond. Toilets at main car parks. Epping Forest Heritage Trust runs the Field Centre.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Ancient pollarded hornbeam forest — the largest accessible ancient wood in the London area. No admission charge. Dogs essentially free to roam in the vast majority of the forest. The sheer scale means it never feels crowded despite being on the edge of London. High search volume potential from East London and Essex.

---

### Delamere Forest, Cheshire

**Size:** 972 hectares managed by Forestry England.
**Admission:** Free entry to the forest; car parking £7/day (Forestry England members free).
**Car Parks:** Three car parks — main visitor centre, Treetops (near the train station — Delamere Station on the Helsby to Northwich line), and Whitefield (off the B5152).

**Off-Lead:** Dogs explicitly welcome throughout the forest with extensive off-lead areas available. The forest's scale means spaces rarely feel crowded.

**Recommended Routes:**
- **The Blakemere Trail** (just under 4 miles): Easy, circular, suitable for all including pushchairs. Passes Blakemere Moss — a large lake/reservoir in the middle of the forest. This is the most popular dog walk route.
- Forest Fitness Trail and Old Pale Trail: More challenging; Old Pale climbs to the highest point in Cheshire.

**Facilities:** Visitor centre, café (dog-friendly outdoor area and water bowls outside), picnic area, accessible toilets. Note: No dog waste bins on the trail — carry out waste (Forestry England policy at Delamere). Closest town: Northwich.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** Relatively flat, well-waymarked forest in an otherwise intensively farmed part of Cheshire. The Blakemere lake provides excellent water access. The train station (Delamere Station) makes this accessible without a car — genuinely useful. Dog water bowls at the café confirm a dog-conscious site. One of the top dog walk destinations in the North West by review volume.

### Savernake Forest, Wiltshire

**Size:** 2,223 acres (approximately 900 hectares) of ancient woodland south of Marlborough.
**Ownership:** Privately owned by the Marquess of Ailesbury but largely open to public access. Managed under a Forestry England agreement.
**Designation:** Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI); registered historic park.

**Off-Lead:** Dogs are generally allowed off-lead throughout the forest. Some sections have signs requesting leads (specific areas not confirmed in research). Well-trained dogs with good recall can roam freely in most of the forest. Wildlife (deer and wild horses) is present — owners must be able to recall dogs immediately.

**Parking:**
- **Postern Hill car park** (SN8 4ND) — main car park, open daily 8am–7pm (except Christmas Day). Toilets open Easter through October half-term.
- Free parking.

**Facilities:** Picnic areas, pathed routes, toilets (seasonal). No café identified within the forest. Marlborough town (SN8 1AA) is 2 miles away with full amenities.

**Route Options:** The Grand Avenue — a 4-mile straight beech avenue running through the heart of the forest — is the iconic feature, planted in the 1700s. The forest offers varied circular routes of 3–8 miles using the network of forest rides and paths.

**What Makes It Distinctive:** One of England's largest and most ancient private forests with public access. The Grand Avenue of beech trees is extraordinary. The SSSI designation reflects the age and ecological richness of the woodland. Genuinely underrated — most dog walk content for Wiltshire focuses on the Marlborough Downs or Stonehenge area. Free parking and open access without NT membership requirements.

# SECTION 5: Dog Facilities Notes

## Dog Waste Bins on the Thames Path

The Thames Path is managed in sections by different local authorities along its 184-mile length. Dog waste bin provision is therefore inconsistent:
- Well-provided in the urban/suburban sections (Richmond, Teddington, Chiswick, Oxford city). Bins are emptied weekly by local authority contractors (Buckinghamshire Council confirmed weekly service in its section).
- Rural sections (upper Thames, Goring Gap, Lechlade) have minimal bin provision — walkers are expected to carry and take waste home or to the nearest urban bin.
- The National Trail itself does not provide bins — these are a local authority responsibility.
- Practical recommendation for Sniffout pages: Include a note on all Thames Path pages advising walkers to carry multiple bags and not rely on bins in rural sections.

## Dog Waste Bins on Canal Towpaths

- **Canal & River Trust** does not directly provide dog waste bins on towpaths as a general policy. Bin provision is the responsibility of adjacent local authorities and parish councils.
- National Trust-managed waterways (River Wey, Brancaster) have confirmed bin provision at key access points.
- Lee Valley Regional Park has bins at main car parks and key entry points.
- Bin provision mid-route on long canal sections (Oxford Canal, Grand Union rural sections) is sparse — carry-out approach is standard.

## Water Sources for Dogs

- **Thames Path:** The river itself provides continuous water access in most non-tidal sections. Dogs should not be encouraged to drink large quantities of still or stagnant river water, but the flowing Thames is generally accessible throughout. Tidal section (below Teddington) has tidal/brackish water concerns — not suitable for drinking.
- **Canal towpaths:** The canal water itself is accessible for dogs but is stagnant and may contain blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) particularly in warm weather (July–September). Dog owners should prevent dogs from drinking canal water during algal bloom season. Check Canal & River Trust algae warnings before visiting in summer. The canal water is fine for paddling and cooling but not for drinking.
- **Cafes and visitor centres:** National Trust properties (River Wey, Brancaster, Morden Hall Park) explicitly provide dog water bowls. Forestry England café at Delamere has water bowls outside.

## Canal & River Trust Policy on Dogs

Published policy (Towpaths for Everyone, January 2024):
- Dogs are NOT required to be on leads on towpaths under CRT general policy.
- In practice, local bylaws in some areas do require leads — walkers should check local signage.
- The CRT Towpath Code ("Share the Space, Drop your Pace") asks all towpath users to be considerate of others.
- Owners are responsible for keeping dogs under close control to prevent chasing wildlife, other dogs, or causing nuisance to cyclists and other walkers.
- Dog fouling must be cleared by owners — CRT does not manage bins but encourages responsible dog ownership.

# SECTION 6: Content Approach Recommendations for Linear Walks

## The Core Challenge

Sniffout's current content model appears to be built around circular walks with a defined start/end car park. Linear walks (Thames Path sections, canal towpaths) do not fit this model. However, they represent a major search volume opportunity and should not be avoided.

## Recommended Page Structures for Linear Walks

**Option A: "Section" pages with a defined shuttle solution**
Frame each page as: "Walk from [A] to [B] — distance, time, return options."
Include return options explicitly:
- Train (where available — Pangbourne/Goring, Henley/Marlow, Guildford/Godalming, Teddington/Richmond all have train connections).
- Bus service numbers and frequency.
- Two-car approach (park one at each end).
- Taxi fare estimate.
This empowers users to complete the walk without needing to retrace steps, and makes the linear format feel practical rather than limiting.

**Option B: "Out and back from [X]" pages**
For shorter sections (Teddington–Richmond, Shepperton–Walton, Lechlade–Kelmscott from one end), frame the page as an out-and-back with a natural turnaround point (a specific pub, a lock, a viewpoint). This suits family/casual walkers who don't need to do the full section. Distances are halved to a more accessible figure.
Example: "Walk to The Trout Inn from Wolvercote and back — 3.5 miles return, 2 hours, off-lead meadows."

**Option C: "Best of the canal" hub page + section sub-pages**
For long canals (Kennet & Avon, Grand Union, Shropshire Union), create:
- A hub page: "Dog Walking on the Kennet & Avon Canal" — overview, best sections summary, facilities guide, seasonal restrictions.
- Individual section pages: "Bath to Bradford-on-Avon by Canal" (9 miles), "The Caen Hill Locks Walk" (5-mile loop), "Hungerford Wharf to Kintbury" (4 miles return).
This captures both broad canary searches and specific section searches, with strong internal linking between pages.

## Page Structure Template for Linear Walks

1. **H1:** [Walk Name] — e.g., "Thames Path: Cookham to Marlow Dog Walk"
2. **Opening paragraph:** Hook — what makes this distinctive. The Bounty pub, the NT meadows, etc.
3. **At a glance box:** Distance / Time / Difficulty / Dog rating / Return options
4. **Getting there:** Parking postcodes, public transport, nearest station.
5. **The Walk:** Narrative description with landmark waypoints. Include where dogs can be off-lead and where leads are needed.
6. **Dog-Friendly Stops:** Named pubs with addresses/postcodes, brief description of dog welcome.
7. **Facilities:** Toilets, cafes, dog waste bins, water access.
8. **Seasonal Notes:** Any restrictions by month.
9. **Return Options:** Train/bus/taxi/two-car detail.
10. **Map/GPX download.**
11. **Nearby walks:** Internal links to other Sniffout pages (for SEO and session depth).

## Handling the "No Fixed Loop" Problem

The absence of a circular route is actually a selling point for some audiences (dog owners who don't want to retrace — the dog gets twice the novelty on a linear). Frame it as: "This walk is best done linear — here's how to return without retracing your steps."

Use photography that celebrates the linear nature — the river ahead, not behind; the towpath stretching into the distance. This reinforces the "going somewhere" narrative rather than the "circle back to the car" model.

## SEO Considerations

Linear canal and Thames Path walks are high-volume, nationally searched terms:
- "Thames Path dog walk" — strong national volume
- "Kennet and Avon Canal dog walk" — strong regional volume (Bristol, Bath, Wiltshire)
- "Regent's Canal dog walk London" — strong London volume
- "Epping Forest dog walk" — very high London/Essex volume
- "Holkham dog walk" — strong national (Norfolk holiday market)

Content should be structured to rank both for the category/canal searches (hub pages) and the specific section searches (section sub-pages). Both contain different user intents: "where can I walk my dog on the K&A?" vs. "walking Bath to Bradford on Avon canal dog."
