## Walk 1: Cookham to Marlow (Thames Path, Berkshire)

Fact check verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS

### Overview

A National Trail section of the Thames Path following the river from Cookham Moor through the National Trust open access land at Cock Marsh - a Site of Special Scientific Interest for lowland wetland - past Bourne End, Hedsor Water, and into Marlow. Cock Marsh is one of the finest unimproved flood meadows in the Chilterns, donated to the National Trust in 1934. The Bounty pub, accessible only on foot or by river, is the headline destination: doggy menu, dog chews, dog water bowls, outdoor riverside seating. A genuine draw for the walk in its own right.

### Distance and format

Linear, one-way: approximately 8.6 to 9.2 miles (14 to 15 km). Variation reflects whether the Winter Hill detour and exact start and end points are included.
Walking time: approximately 3.5 to 4 hours.
Source: AllTrails (two separate listings), National Trails website.

### Start point and parking

Start: Cookham Moor, National Trust car park, The Moor, Cookham. Navigation postcode: SL6 9SG (confirmed via Parkopedia and National Trust).
NT members: free.
Non-NT members: £2 for stays over 30 minutes. Under 30 minutes is free.
Note: the 30-minute free threshold applies before charging begins.

End / return parking: Higginson Park car park, Marlow. Postcode SL7 2AZ area. Confirmed as endpoint parking option.

Cookham station postcode for navigation: SL6 9BP (not SL6 9BN - corrected from research, source: GWR station page).

### Public transport

Cookham station is served by Great Western Railway on the Marlow Branch Line, connecting to Maidenhead. Confirmed operator and route.

Return from Marlow: GWR train from Marlow to Maidenhead (approximately 20 minutes), then Elizabeth line to Paddington. Services approximately hourly.
Source: GWR, Trainline.

OMIT - bus return: No specific bus route number confirmed from an operator source. Research references "Thames Valley bus services" and a Marlow-Cookham/Maidenhead bus but no route number is verified. Do not publish as a named return option.

### Return options

1. Walk back the same route (Thames Path south bank).
2. Train from Marlow to Maidenhead, then Elizabeth line. Verify current timetable at gwr.com before visiting.

### Off-lead rules

CORRECTED - Cock Marsh and Cookham Common: Dogs must be kept on short leads or under close control on the National Trust commons at Cookham Moor and Cock Marsh. This is the NT's own guidance for Maidenhead and Cookham Commons, due to cattle grazing and ground-nesting birds. The research pack claim that "NT does not impose lead requirements here in normal circumstances" and that dogs "can be off-lead throughout the year" is INCORRECT.

Near Marlow lock and the busy town approaches: leads recommended. Confirmed in research.

Source: nationaltrust.org.uk, Maidenhead and Cookham Commons dog walking page.

### Livestock and seasonal restrictions

Cock Marsh cattle grazing: Cattle graze seasonally from approximately April to November. Dogs must be on leads when cattle are present. The research claim that "there is no livestock typically on Cock Marsh itself" is INCORRECT and contradicted by NT and conservation management sources.
Source: Wild Maidenhead, NT conservation notes, Freshwater Habitats Trust.

Ground-nesting birds: SSSI wetland sections. Keep to paths March to July.
Source: confirmed in both research files and fact check.

### Dog waste bins

No confirmed bin locations mid-route from NT or local authority sources. The Thames Path rural section between Cookham and Marlow has minimal bin provision.
Advice for walk page: carry waste bags, do not rely on bins on the rural section. Bins available at Cookham village and Marlow town.

### Water for dogs

River access: Multiple informal grassy bank access points to the Thames along this section, including near The Bounty pub area. Dogs can wade and swim safely (non-tidal section above Teddington Lock).
Drinking water: The Bounty provides water bowls. The White Oak pub has water. Flowing Thames accessible but dogs should not drink from standing or stagnant sections.

### Terrain and paw safety

Mix of grass, compacted path, and some tarmac. Passable year-round. Can be muddy in meadow sections October to March. No paw burn risk specific to this route.

OMIT - stile-free status: No authoritative confirmation from NT or National Trails for this specific section. Thames Path generally uses kissing gates on this stretch but full stile-free status for large dogs is not confirmed. Do not publish stile-free claim.

### Dog-friendly pubs and cafes

**The Bounty** (Cookmarsh, SL6 0JG area): Dogs allowed inside on lead. Doggy menu. Dog chews available for a charity donation. Water bowls. Accessible on foot or by river only - no road access. Open for food and drink. 2025-2026 TripAdvisor reviews confirm continued operation and dog-friendly status.
Source: TripAdvisor 2026, inapub.co.uk, dogfriendly.co.uk, dogfuriendly.com.

**The White Oak** (Cookham, SL6 9QE): Dogs welcome in bar. Free treats. Confirmed 2025-2026.
Source: thewhiteoak.pub official website, Rover dog-friendly pub awards, TripAdvisor 2026.

OMIT - The Two Brewers and Crown Hotel (Marlow): No 2025-2026 primary source confirmation of dog access found. Do not publish dog access claims for these venues.

### Safety flags

Road crossing: The Thames Path near Cookham makes a diversion away from the riverbank near Cliveden (no public ferry, private towpath section). This diversion takes walkers briefly onto roads. Flag on walk page.
Tidal: Non-tidal throughout (section is above Teddington Lock). No tidal risk.
Cattle: See livestock section. Leads required when cattle present (approximately April to November).
Ground-nesting birds: March to July, keep to paths in SSSI wetland sections.

## Walk 2: Teddington to Richmond (Thames Path, SW London)

Fact check verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS

### Overview

A scenic Thames Path section from Teddington Lock - the tidal limit of the Thames - to Richmond Bridge via Petersham. One of the most scenic stretches on the entire river, combining tidal Thames drama at the lock, the open water meadow character of the Ham riverside, and the Georgian charm of Richmond town. Ideal as a half-day walk with train return. Accessible year-round. Note: a statutory dog ban is in effect at Petersham Meadows from 1 April to 31 October (see Off-lead rules).

### Distance and format

Linear, one-way: approximately 5.5 to 6.5 km (3.5 to 4 miles). The research figure of approximately 5 km / 3 miles is at the shorter end - correct figure is 5.5 to 6.5 km.
Source: walkthethames.co.uk, AllTrails.

### Start point and parking

Start (Teddington): Cedar Road car park, TW11 9AN. 22 bays, Mon-Sat 8am-6:30pm, chargeable. Teddington Pool car park also available: TW11 8EZ, 42 bays, Mon-Fri 8:30am-6:30pm, Sat 8:30am-5pm, chargeable. Free parking at Bushy Park (short walk to river).
Source: richmond.gov.uk/teddington_area_car_parks, Parkopedia.

End (Richmond): Mostly resident-only parking zones in Richmond. Public transport strongly preferred for this end. Richmond station postcode: TW9 2NA.

### Public transport

Teddington station operator: South Western Railway, operating as DfT Operator Ltd (the brand name South Western Railway continues to be used). Zone 6. Shepperton Branch Line and Kingston Loop Line, services to London Waterloo. Note: as of 25 May 2025 the franchise transferred to DfT Operator Limited - do not describe operator as FirstGroup or MTR.
Source: nationalrail.co.uk.

Return from Richmond: Richmond station (South Western Railway / DfT Operator, Zone 4), direct services to London Waterloo. Also District Line (TfL) and London Overground connections at Richmond.

### Return options

1. Train from Richmond station to Teddington station (verify current timetable at nationalrail.co.uk before visiting).

### Off-lead rules

CRITICAL CORRECTION - Petersham Meadows: A Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO) issued by the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames imposes a TOTAL BAN on dogs in Petersham Meadows from 1 April to 31 October each year. This is NOT a leads-required rule - it is a complete prohibition on dogs. Violating a PSPO is a criminal offence. The National Trust manages the meadows under a lease from Richmond Council and enforces this ban.

This means the Teddington to Richmond south bank walk is NOT a continuous dog walk between 1 April and 31 October without a diversion.

Alternative route when ban is in force: At the point where the Thames Path approaches Petersham Meadows, dog walkers must take the riverside diversion - left at Petersham Nursery, following the track to the river and along the Thames Path. The walk page must state this diversion clearly and prominently.

The walk page must include this exact advisory: "Dogs are completely banned from Petersham Meadows 1 April to 31 October (PSPO enforced by Richmond Council). Use the riverside diversion via the Thames Path - follow signs at the Petersham Nursery junction."

Ham Lands (accessible from north bank via Teddington Lock footbridges): Dogs welcome off-lead throughout. No formal lead requirements. Confirmed HIGH confidence.
Source: Inner London Ramblers, richmond.gov.uk (Petersham Meadows page), Citizen Space PSPO consultation documents.

### Livestock and seasonal restrictions

Petersham Meadows cattle grazing: Cattle (Angus cross Hereford heifers) graze Petersham Meadows April to October. The dog ban is directly connected to this conservation grazing programme managed by the National Trust.

Dogs are banned entirely from the meadows April to October under the PSPO - not merely required on leads.
Source: Petersham Environment Trust, Richmond Society.

### Dog waste bins

Confirmed present throughout the Richmond and Teddington riverside corridor. London Borough of Richmond upon Thames and Royal Borough of Kingston maintain bins. Well-maintained urban-fringe section.

### Water for dogs

Thames access: Non-tidal above Teddington Lock. Dogs can paddle safely at multiple points upstream from the lock on the south bank approach from Teddington.

Important: Teddington Lock is the exact tidal limit. The weir and lock area can be hazardous for dogs - keep dogs away from the weir structure. Do not encourage swimming near the weir.

Below Teddington Lock the river is tidal. Do not encourage swimming or paddling in the tidal section.

Water bowls: The Lass O'Richmond Hill confirmed providing water bowls and treats.

### Terrain and paw safety

Well-surfaced and flat throughout. Mix of tarmac and compacted path. Suitable year-round.

### Dog-friendly pubs and cafes

**The Lass O'Richmond Hill** (8 Queens Road, Richmond, TW10 6JJ): Dogs described as "dog-loving" by the pub. Treats and water bowls provided. Pub dog (Pickles) on site. Dogs welcome in bar. Young's pub. 2025-2026 confirmed.
Note: this pub is at the top of Richmond Hill near Terrace Gardens, not directly on the riverside path - it is a pre or post walk stop depending on route.
Source: lassorichmondhill.co.uk, dogfuriendly.com, TripAdvisor 2026.

**The Dysart Petersham** (Petersham Road, Richmond, TW10 7AA): Dogs confirmed welcome, water bowls and towels for muddy paws provided. However the Dysart is now a Michelin-starred restaurant. Simply calling in for a drink without eating is discouraged. Frame as a post-walk dining venue with dog access, not a casual pub stop.
Source: TripAdvisor, SquareMeal, OpenTable.

**The White Hart** (1 Riverside, Twickenham, TW1 3DX): Dogs confirmed welcome, riverside, multiple 2025-2026 sources confirm access. NOTE: This pub is on the north (Twickenham/Middlesex) bank. It is NOT on the standard south bank Teddington-Richmond route. Only relevant to walkers using the north bank alternative via Ham Lands. Clarify this on the walk page.
Source: confirmed in research, Pure Pet Food dog-friendly pubs guide.

OMIT - The Waterman's Arms: This pub is near Kew Bridge and is in the wrong section context for this walk. Do not include.

### Safety flags

CRITICAL - Petersham Meadows PSPO: Total dog ban 1 April to 31 October. Criminal offence. Alternative route required. See Off-lead rules section above.
Tidal limit: Teddington Lock is the tidal limit. Weir area hazardous - keep dogs away from the weir structure.
Cyclists: Popular path, especially on weekends. Keep dogs under control.

### Items omitted pending verification

- The Waterman's Arms (wrong section, excluded)
- Any claims about Petersham Meadows being a "leads required" zone (incorrect - it is a total ban under PSPO)

## Walk 3: Oxleas Wood and Shooters Hill (SE London)

Fact check verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS

### Overview

133.5 hectares of connected ancient broadleaf woodlands and meadows in SE London: Eltham Common, Castle Wood (with Severndroog Castle folly), Jack Wood, Oxleas Meadow, Oxleas Wood, Deansfield, Shepherdleas Wood, and Falconwood Field. One of the largest areas of ancient woodland remaining in Greater London. Dogs off-lead throughout the woodland and meadow areas. Part of the Green Chain Walk network. Bluebells in spring; good views over London from Severndroog Castle tower.

### Distance and format

Circular. Standard circuits: 3 to 4 miles (5 to 6.5 km).
- Oxleas and Shepherdleas Woods Circular: 4.8 km / 3 miles
- Oxleas, Shepherdleas and Jack Wood Circular: 5.8 km / 3.6 miles
- Falconwood Circular (Oxleas Wood and Shooters Hill): 6.3 km / 3.9 miles

CORRECTED: The research range of "2 to 6 miles" overstates the upper end. The standard circuits are 3 to 4 miles. The "6 miles" upper claim is not supported by route data.
Source: AllTrails (multiple Oxleas circuits), walkingclub.org.uk Falconwood circular.

### Start point and parking

Free car parks off Shooters Hill Road (SE18 area). Main car park postcode: SE18 3JA (confirmed from multiple sources). The main Shooters Hill Road car park fills on busy sunny days - arrive early for weekends.
Source: Oxleas Wood official site, AccessAble.

### Public transport

OMIT - Falconwood station and bus routes: Specific walking times from Falconwood station (Southeastern Railway, zone 4) are given variously as 10 to 40 minutes in different sources. Bus routes 89 and 486 serve Shooters Hill but have not been verified from TfL source. Do not publish public transport details without operator-source verification.

### Return options

Circular walk - no return transport needed.

### Off-lead rules

Dogs welcome off-lead throughout the woodland and meadow areas. No formal lead requirements except near the cafe enclosure (see below). Royal Borough of Greenwich manages the site.
Source: royalgreenwich.gov.uk, visitor reports, Friends of Oxleas Woodlands.

Cafe enclosure: The Oxleas Wood Cafe is in a fenced dog-free enclosure (except guide dogs). Tables immediately outside the fence are available to dog owners and staff serve outside tables.
Source: AccessAble, multiple visitor reviews.

### Livestock and seasonal restrictions

No livestock on Oxleas Wood. No lead requirements related to cattle or sheep.

OMIT - ground-nesting bird restrictions: No specific ground-nesting bird restriction confirmed from Royal Borough of Greenwich for Oxleas Wood. Not a publishable restriction without authority source.

### Dog waste bins

Dog waste bins present at main entry points from Shooters Hill Road and in the cafe area. Royal Borough of Greenwich maintains bins. Confirmed at main access points.
Source: Greenwich council site, AccessAble.

### Water for dogs

Drinking water: Water available outside the cafe (staff serve outside tables). No confirmed separate dog water tap.

OMIT - swimming / water access: No safe water access point for dogs confirmed within Oxleas Wood. The woods do not border a river. No pond access confirmed from managing body. Advise no swimming available on this walk.

### Terrain and paw safety

Ancient broadleaf woodland on varied terrain. Shooters Hill is elevated ground - some slopes. Mix of natural woodland paths and tarmac paths near cafe and car park. Can be muddy after rain.

Hot weather paw risk: Tarmac paths near the cafe and car park area can be hot in direct summer sun. Natural woodland paths are shaded and lower risk.

### Dog-friendly pubs and cafes

**Severndroog Castle Tea Room** (Castle Wood, SE18): Dogs welcome in the tea room on the ground floor and in the courtyard. Dog water bowls, treats, and dog ice cream on sale. Thursday to Sunday, Tea Room 9am to 3:30pm; Viewing Platform 10am to 3:30pm Sundays only.
Note: Do not repeat the claim that this is "the only dog-friendly castle in London" as a stated fact. This is a marketing or editorial claim from The Londog blog and is not independently verifiable. It can be quoted as a popular description if clearly attributed.
Source: severndroogcastle.org.uk, TripAdvisor 2025-2026.

**Oxleas Wood Cafe**: Open daily 8am to 5pm (check for extended summer hours). Hot meals, sandwiches, cakes, ice cream. Dogs outside the fence only - not inside. A reliable stop for all-day visits.
Source: livewellgreenwich.org.uk, AccessAble.

### Safety flags

Shooters Hill Road crossing: The A207 Shooters Hill Road bisects access to some sections of the woodland complex. Dogs on lead when crossing this busy road.
Cyclists: Mixed pedestrian and cyclist use on some wider paths, particularly near the cafe. Keep dogs under close control on the wider tracks.

## Walk 4: Holkham Beach and Pinewoods (North Norfolk)

Fact check verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS

### Overview

One of the widest beaches in England, with an exceptional combination of open tidal beach, vast dunes, and ancient pinewoods backdrop. Dogs welcome on the beach year-round. Over 70% of the beach is accessible off-lead even during the seasonal restricted zone (1 April to 31 August) when nesting shorebirds require protection in roped-off sections. A destination walk rather than a local one - plan for a half or full day. Tidal bay: check tide times before visiting.

### Distance and format

Linear or out-and-back. From the car park, a 1-mile walk through the pinewoods leads to the beach. Standard out-and-back (car park, pinewoods, 2 miles along beach, return): approximately 6 miles total. Car park to Wells-next-the-Sea circular: approximately 4.6 miles / 7.4 km.
Source: AllTrails, Countryfile.

### Start point and parking

Lady Anne's Drive car park, Holkham. Postcode: NR23 1RG (confirmed correct).
Charges (confirmed 2024-2025 tariff from holkham.co.uk):
- Up to 2 hours: £3.60
- Up to 4 hours: £7.00
- Over 4 hours / all day: £13.50

CORRECTED: The research figure of approximately £9 for a full day is INCORRECT. The all-day rate is £13.50. This is a significant difference that would mislead visitors.

Car park open: 6am to 9pm daily.
Source: holkham.co.uk parking page, Parkopedia.

### Public transport

OMIT - Coasthopper bus: No rail station at Holkham. The Coasthopper bus (Konectbus / Sanders Coaches) runs along the North Norfolk coast and stops near Holkham. However this has not been verified from the operator's current schedule. Holkham is primarily a car-dependent destination.

For the walk page: note that Holkham is best reached by car, and that a coastal bus service operates on this route but walkers should verify current routes and timetable directly before visiting.

### Return options

Out-and-back (standard). Alternatively, one-way to Wells-next-the-Sea for those with transport at both ends.

### Off-lead rules

Dogs welcome on Holkham Beach year-round. Over 70% of the beach remains freely accessible for off-lead walking.

Seasonal restricted zone (1 April to 31 August): Dogs must be on leads in the roped-off sections around Holkham Gap (90 hectares) and Gun Hill on the western edge (20 hectares). These zones protect ground-nesting shorebirds. Clear signage and beach wardens are in place during the restricted season.
Source: holkham.co.uk dog zoning page, Barking Bugle, Fakenham Times.

Pinewoods (Lady Anne's Drive entrance): CORRECTED to year-round rule. Dogs must be kept on leads when walking through the pinewoods at all times. Predominant official guidance states this without seasonal qualification. Publish as a year-round requirement.
Source: holkham.co.uk FAQ, walkiees.co.uk Holkham dog walk page.

### Livestock and seasonal restrictions

No livestock on Holkham Beach.

Ground-nesting birds: Seasonal dog restrictions (1 April to 31 August) specifically protect ground-nesting shorebirds including lapwings, ringed plovers, and oystercatchers breeding in the dune and beach transition zone.

### Dog waste bins

Bins at the car park and at the Lookout cafe. No bins on the open beach itself.
Advice for walk page: carry waste bags for the beach section.

### Water for dogs

The Lookout cafe provides water bowls for dogs. Confirmed.

Cafe hours: CORRECTED. Opens from approximately 10am. Hours vary by season (mid-winter as low as 10am to 3pm). Do not publish the research figure of 9:30am as it is not confirmed. Publish as: "open from approximately 10am, hours vary by season - check holkham.co.uk before visiting."
Source: holkham.co.uk opening times, visitnorthnorfolk.com.

Sea swimming: Holkham Bay is a wide, shallow tidal beach. Generally safe conditions for dog paddling in calm conditions. Tidal - see safety flags.

No blue-green algae risk: Tidal saltwater location. Not applicable.

### Terrain and paw safety

Pinewoods path: Broad sandy compacted path, suitable for most dogs and pushchairs.
Beach: Open sand. Some boardwalk sections through dunes.

Hot weather paw burn: Sand at Holkham can become very hot in direct summer sun. Walk dogs on the beach during cooler parts of the day in hot weather. The tide-cooled wet sand near the shoreline is safer for paws in summer.

### Dog-friendly pubs and cafes

OMIT - The Victoria at Holkham: The Victoria is on the Holkham Estate and is the natural pre or post walk venue (holkham.co.uk/stay/the-victoria). However dog access policy has not been confirmed from 2025-2026 primary sources. Do not include without verifying directly from the estate.

The Lookout cafe: dogs outside only (inside the RIBA Award-winning building is not dog-accessible), water bowls outside. Good for provisions.

### Safety flags

TIDAL RISK - HIGH PRIORITY: Holkham Bay is a wide, flat tidal beach. The tide comes in very rapidly across the flat sand and can isolate walkers who have walked far from the shore access point. This is a documented safety hazard at Holkham. Walk page must advise: check tide times before visiting, do not walk across the tidal flats when the tide is coming in, return to the shore access point before the tide rises. Check tide tables at bbc.co.uk/weather/coast-and-sea or the Environment Agency before visiting.
Source: General Norfolk coastal safety guidance, wellsguide.com.

Sand paw burn: hot weather advisory (see terrain section).

### Items omitted pending verification

- Coasthopper bus timetable and stops (operator source not confirmed)
- The Victoria at Holkham dog policy (no 2025-2026 primary source)
- All-day parking at £9 (corrected to £13.50 - old figure to be discarded entirely)
- 9:30am Lookout cafe opening time (corrected to approximately 10am)

## Walk 5: Epping Forest - Connaught Water Circuit (Essex / East London)

Fact check verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS

### Overview

2,400 hectares of ancient pollarded hornbeam forest straddling the Essex and East London border, managed by the City of London Corporation. One of the finest examples of ancient pollarded hornbeam woodland in Europe, with SSSI, SAC, and Special Protection Area designations. The Connaught Water circuit centres on a large ornamental lake and surrounding forest. Dogs under effective control do not need to be on a lead in most of the forest. Conservation cattle grazing in some zones - leads required near cattle. Blue-green algae risk at Connaught Water in warm weather - see safety flags.

### Distance and format

Circular, Connaught Water-centred: approximately 4 to 5 miles (6.5 to 8 km).

CORRECTED: The research figure of 7.1 miles (3 hours) from Loughton refers to a broader circuit starting from Loughton station that extends beyond Connaught Water. The Connaught Water-specific circuits are significantly shorter.
- Epping Forest and Connaught Water Circular (AllTrails): 7.7 km / 4.8 miles
- Willow Trail around Connaught Water only: 2.5 miles / 4 km

Extend to 7+ miles by combining with Chingford Plain and the wider High Beach area.
Source: AllTrails, City of London Connaught Water page.

### Start point and parking

Connaught Water car park: Rangers Road, Chingford. Postcode: E4 7QH.

CORRECTED: The research postcode IG10 3SZ is INCORRECT for Connaught Water. IG10 3SZ is a Loughton postcode and would misdirect drivers to the wrong location. Use E4 7QH.

CORRECTED: Connaught Water car park is NOT free. The City of London Corporation now charges for parking here.
Charges (Connaught Water, confirmed from cityoflondon.gov.uk):
- Up to 1 hour: £1.75
- Up to 2 hours: £3.00
- 2 to 4 hours: £4.50
- Full day (over 4 hours): £7.00
- Motorbikes: free
- Blue Badge holders: free (registration required)

Do not publish "52 free car parks" as a current fact. The forest has approximately 52 car parks in total but the City of London now charges at 36 of them. The blanket "free" claim is outdated.
Source: cityoflondon.gov.uk/car-parking-at-epping-forest.

High Beach Visitor Centre car park: IG10 4AF.
CORRECTED: Research claims "approximately £6 for up to 6 hours." Actual tariff: up to 4 hours £4.50, full day (over 4 hours) £7.00. The £6 figure is not consistent with the published tariff.

### Public transport

Chingford station (Elizabeth line / Greater Anglia): approximately 20 to 30 minutes walk from Connaught Water car park. Nearest rail option.

Buckhurst Hill (Central line): approximately 35 minutes walk from Connaught Water area.

Note on walk times: Various sources give 20 to 40 minutes from Chingford station. Publish as "approximately 20 to 30 minutes from Chingford station" until on-the-ground verification is available.
Source: National Rail, confirmed in research.

### Return options

Circular walk - no return transport needed.

### Off-lead rules

City of London Corporation confirms dogs are NOT required to be on a lead in Epping Forest, provided they respond reliably to recall. If a dog does not respond to recall, it must be kept on a lead. In practice, the vast majority of the forest is accessible off-lead.
Source: cityoflondon.gov.uk/dog-walking-in-epping-forest.

Conservation grazing areas (cattle): Cattle are present in designated zones using GPS collar containment. Dogs must not chase or disturb cattle. Keep dogs on leads when passing near cattle. The City of London asks visitors to "pass cows as widely and quietly as possible." Cattle locations vary and can be checked via the City of London cattle tracker before visiting.

Useful resource: City of London real-time cattle location tracker at cityoflondon.gov.uk. Include this on the walk page.
Source: cityoflondon.gov.uk/grazing-in-epping-forest.

### Livestock and seasonal restrictions

Conservation cattle grazing programme active. Cattle have grazed in Epping Forest for over 1,000 years. GPS containment collars in use since 2011. Cattle locations vary - check tracker before visiting.

### Dog waste bins

Bins at main car parks including Connaught Water and High Beach Visitor Centre. Mid-forest bin provision is limited. Carry waste bags for routes away from the car parks.

### Water for dogs

Connaught Water (the lake): Accessible at the lake edge. HOWEVER: blue-green algae safety risk - see safety flags. Do not encourage dogs to drink from or swim in Connaught Water without checking current water notices.

Forest streams and ponds: The forest has numerous streams and ponds beyond Connaught Water. Small forest streams provide water access points.

### Terrain and paw safety

Ancient woodland, mix of natural earth paths and some compacted gravel tracks. The path around Connaught Water is hard and level (confirmed accessible). Forest paths away from the lake are natural woodland surfaces and can be uneven. Can be very muddy October to March.

### Dog-friendly pubs and cafes

OMIT - specific pub for Connaught Water circuit: No dog-friendly pub confirmed from 2025-2026 sources for the Connaught Water circuit specifically. The Royal Forest hotel area and tea hut at Pillow Mounds (High Beach area) are nearby but dog access not confirmed. Do not include without direct verification.

### Safety flags

BLUE-GREEN ALGAE at Connaught Water: Connaught Water has a documented history of blue-green algae outbreaks. In 2018, the City of London Corporation placed precautionary warning signs following a suspected outbreak and requested an Environment Agency investigation. Blue-green algae can be fatal to dogs. The risk recurs seasonally in warm weather. Dog owners must check the City of London's current notices before allowing dogs to enter or drink from Connaught Water, particularly July to September. This must be prominently flagged on the walk page.
Source: City of London press release (news.cityoflondon.gov.uk).

DEER - chasing hazard: Fallow deer are present throughout Epping Forest. Dogs with poor recall may chase deer. This is both dangerous (deer can kick and injure dogs) and illegal under Epping Forest byelaws. Flag on walk page.

Cyclists: Cyclists share many of the wider forest tracks including routes around Connaught Water. Keep dogs under control on main tracks.

### Items omitted pending verification

- Precise walking time from Chingford station to Connaught Water (range not fully verified on-the-ground)
- "52 free car parks" claim (outdated - charge now applies at 36 car parks including Connaught Water)
