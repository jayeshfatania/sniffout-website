## CATEGORY A: Thames Path Sections

### A1. Kingston upon Thames to Teddington Lock (via Canbury Gardens and Ham Lands)

**Start Point**
Canbury Gardens, Kingston upon Thames. The nearest car park is on Lower Ham Road, KT2 5AU (Canbury Gardens car park, also signed as Lower Ham Road car park). This is a small riverside car park immediately adjacent to the gardens. Alternative parking: Kingston town centre multi-storeys (John Lewis car park, KT1 1QZ, or Bentall Centre). Nearest station: Kingston (National Rail, South Western Railway), approximately 10–12 minutes walk south along the river to Canbury Gardens. Bus: multiple services to Kingston town centre.

**Distance and Format**
Kingston Bridge to Teddington Lock (south/Surrey bank, via Canbury Gardens): approximately 3.5–4 miles (5.6–6.4 km) one way, linear. The walk is almost entirely flat. The standard outing for locals is an out-and-back of approximately 7–8 miles, or a circular using the Teddington Lock footbridge to cross to the Middlesex/north bank and return via Ham Lands and Ham bank. The circular is approximately 8–9 miles total depending on detours.

**Canbury Gardens: Specific Detail**
Canbury Gardens is a public park on the Surrey/south bank of the Thames, running approximately 500m between Richmond Road and the boatyards near Teddington Road. It is a long, narrow riverside park with mature trees, benches, and a bandstand. Facilities within and adjacent to the gardens include public toilets (at the south end near the car park off Lower Ham Road), a cafe (the Canbury Arms is nearby but the gardens themselves have had a small refreshment offer at points), and a children's play area.

Dog waste bins: Confirmed present at the main entrances and at intervals within Canbury Gardens. Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames (RBK) maintains bins along this stretch. Bins are located at the Lower Ham Road entrance, at the bandstand area, and at the north end near Richmond Road. The exact bin count changes with council maintenance cycles but multiple bins are present within the gardens as of recent reporting.

Dogs: Canbury Gardens is dog-friendly throughout. Dogs are permitted on leads or off-lead. There are no formal off-lead-only zones but in practice the open grass areas are used freely by dog walkers. This is one of the most popular local dog-walking parks in Kingston.

**From Canbury Gardens to Teddington Lock: Route Description**
From Canbury Gardens, the Thames Path continues north (upstream, towards Teddington). The path leaves Canbury Gardens at its northern end and continues along the riverbank past boatyards, through a section past the Thames Ditton/Hampton Court island area on the opposite bank, and on to the approach of Teddington Lock. The surface is a mix of tarmac path, compacted gravel, and some grassy sections. It is pushchair and wheelchair accessible for most sections. The path is well-signed as the Thames Path National Trail.

**River Access for Dogs (South Bank, Kingston Bridge to Teddington Lock)**
Dogs can access the water at multiple informal points along this stretch:
- Within Canbury Gardens: the riverbank has several informal grassy access points to the water's edge. Not formal beach/slipway but dogs can wade in at multiple points.
- Between Canbury Gardens and Teddington: there are further informal bank access points where the path comes close to the water's edge. The bank is predominantly grassy with some muddy patches.
- At Teddington Lock weir: there is an area near the lock and weir where dogs commonly access the water from the bank.

Formal slipways and hard access points are fewer on this stretch than further downstream but informal grassy bank access is frequent.

**Ham Lands: Access and Detail**
Ham Lands is a Local Nature Reserve and open space managed by the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames, on the Middlesex (north) bank of the Thames between Teddington Lock and Ham. It is NOT directly accessible from the Kingston/south bank by bridge other than at Teddington Lock. To access Ham Lands from the Kingston side, walkers cross at Teddington Lock footbridge (at Teddington Lock itself — there are two footbridges crossing to the Middlesex bank at the lock: one over the weir/lasher section and one over the lock). These footbridges are free to cross and open to the public.

Ham Lands character: An extensive area of rough grassland, scrub, and former gravel workings. It is a Local Nature Reserve and is managed for biodiversity (wildflowers, invertebrates). It is NOT a formal dog exclusion zone. Dogs are permitted throughout Ham Lands. There is no livestock on Ham Lands. The area is used extensively by local dog walkers and is genuinely off-lead territory throughout — the absence of roads (the area is bounded by the river and Ham Street/Riverside Drive) makes it very suitable for off-lead dogs.

Ham Lands restrictions: No formal dogs-on-leads requirements known for this site. Ground-nesting bird areas may be noted on signage seasonally (spring/early summer) but this is not uniformly enforced and the LB Richmond signage asks for considerate dog walking rather than imposing lead requirements. There is no livestock. Nettles and rough scrub are common from May onwards.

**Circular Route Feasibility**
The circular is well-established among locals:
- Kingston Bridge → south bank (Thames Path via Canbury Gardens) → Teddington Lock (approximately 3.5 miles)
- Cross Teddington Lock footbridges to Middlesex bank
- Return south via Ham Lands and Ham riverside path → Ham House area → cross back to Kingston via Richmond Bridge or continue to Richmond

Note: A pure return from Teddington Lock via the north bank back to Kingston Bridge is not direct — the north bank route goes to Ham, then you either continue to Richmond (adding significant distance) or there is no direct footbridge back to Kingston except by returning to Teddington Lock or going to Richmond Bridge. The more accurate circular for a Kingston-based walk is therefore:
- Kingston Bridge → Teddington Lock (south bank, 3.5 miles)
- Cross at Teddington Lock to Ham Lands
- Walk south through Ham Lands to Ham riverside path
- Continue to Richmond Bridge
- Cross Richmond Bridge back to Surrey bank
- Walk south bank back to Kingston Bridge along the Thames Path (approx 2 miles)
Total circular approximately 9–10 miles.

A shorter version: some locals do Kingston Bridge south bank to Teddington Lock, cross, walk Ham Lands briefly, and return via same footbridge — this is an out-and-back with a Ham Lands detour, approximately 8 miles total.

**The White Swan (Teddington)**
Address: Riverside, Teddington, TW11 9ND (also known as the White Swan, Ferry Road, Teddington). This pub is on the Middlesex bank near Teddington Lock. It is a riverside pub with outdoor seating directly on the Thames bank. Dogs are confirmed welcome outside in the beer garden/riverside seating area. Whether dogs are permitted inside varies by report — multiple sources confirm dogs welcome in the bar area as well as outside but this should be verified as pub policies change. Water bowls: not confirmed from primary sources but common at riverside pubs of this type.

**The Anglers (Teddington)**
Address: 3 Broom Road, Teddington, TW11 9NR. This is a larger pub/dining establishment near Teddington Lock and the weir. It is riverside with a large outdoor terrace. Dogs are confirmed welcome outside (riverside terrace). Reports of dogs being permitted in the bar area inside are less consistent — this appears to be a pub where inside dog access depends on time of day and which staff member is on. The outside terrace is the reliable dog-friendly option. Food served all day. This pub is very popular on sunny weekends.

**CONFIDENCE: A1**
- Canbury Gardens bin locations: MEDIUM (general presence confirmed, specific locations based on RBK maintenance records and user reports)
- White Swan indoor dog access: MEDIUM (outside confirmed, inside requires direct verification)
- Anglers indoor dog access: LOW-MEDIUM (variable reports)
- Ham Lands nesting bird restrictions: MEDIUM (seasonal requests noted but not formal exclusion)
- Ham Lands circular return route via Richmond: HIGH (well established)

### A2. Teddington to Richmond (Both Banks)

**Overview**
A beautiful section of the Thames, one of the most scenic on the entire river. Distance from Teddington Lock to Richmond Bridge is approximately 3.5–4 miles (5.6–6.4 km) along the Surrey/south bank. The north (Middlesex) bank through Ham and past Marble Hill Park offers an alternative.

**Surrey/South Bank: Character and Route**
From Teddington Lock, the south bank Thames Path passes through Teddington riverside, continues past the boatyards and into Petersham Meadows, then climbs through Petersham village and enters Richmond along the river. The path is varied: riverbank tow path, then open meadow, then entering Richmond riverside.

**Petersham Meadows: Critical Dog Information**
Petersham Meadows is a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) and is grazed by cattle (and historically sheep) for conservation purposes. Cattle are present on Petersham Meadows typically from spring through autumn — the exact dates vary year to year based on the grazing management programme run by the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames. Cattle are generally put out in April/May and removed in October/November. During grazing periods, dogs MUST be kept on leads in Petersham Meadows. This is a statutory requirement in a grazed area and the signage makes this clear. Dogs off-lead in cattle fields are both dangerous (cattle can charge at dogs and their owners) and potentially legally problematic.

There is no seasonal closure of the Meadows to walkers or dogs, but the on-lead requirement during grazing is firmly in place when cattle are present.

**Middlesex/North Bank: Ham Bank and Marble Hill**
From Teddington Lock, crossing to the Middlesex bank, the route passes through Ham Lands (see A1), then along the Ham riverbank. Further south (towards Richmond) the path passes along the edge of Ham House grounds (National Trust), then through the area in front of Marble Hill House and Marble Hill Park.

Marble Hill Park: A large open parkland managed by English Heritage. Dogs are welcome throughout. The park is open access. Dogs may be off-lead in the park itself.

Ham House (NT): The Thames Path passes in front of the house along the riverbank. Dogs are welcome in the grounds on leads. The NT cafe at Ham House: dogs are welcome outside in the courtyard garden seating area. Dogs inside the house or formal gardens are not permitted.

**Hammerton's Ferry**
Between Ham/Marble Hill bank and Twickenham/St Margarets bank, Hammerton's Ferry is a small rowing boat ferry operating seasonally (typically Easter to October, weekends and bank holidays year-round, daily in summer). Cost is a few pounds per person. Dogs travel free or at low cost. This ferry is NOT on the Richmond direct route but is relevant for combinations with Twickenham or walks toward Richmond Bridge.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs: Teddington to Richmond**

The Lass O'Richmond Hill: Address: The Lass O'Richmond Hill, 8 Queens Road, Richmond, TW10 6JJ. This is not directly on the river but is at the top of Richmond Hill, near Terrace Gardens. Dogs confirmed welcome. It is a Fuller's pub with a traditional interior. Dogs in the bar area confirmed from multiple sources. This is more of a pre/post walk pub depending on route.

The White Hart: Address: The White Hart, 1 Riverside, Twickenham, TW1 3DX (note: there are multiple White Harts in the area — the one relevant to this route is in Twickenham by the river). This White Hart is on the south bank of the river at Twickenham — relevant if approaching from the Middlesex/Twickenham bank. Dogs confirmed welcome at the riverside pub. This is one of the better-known dog-friendly Thames pubs in this stretch.

The Dysart Petersham: Address: Petersham Road, Richmond, TW10 7AA. An upmarket pub/restaurant in Petersham. Dogs permitted in the bar/snug area confirmed. This is on the route if walking the south bank from Teddington through Petersham.

**Distance Summary**
- Teddington Lock to Richmond Bridge (south bank, via Petersham Meadows): approximately 3.5–4 miles
- Teddington Lock to Richmond (north bank, via Ham Lands, Ham House, Marble Hill): approximately 4–4.5 miles
- Combined circular via both banks using Richmond Bridge and Teddington Lock footbridge: approximately 8 miles

**CONFIDENCE: A2**
- Petersham Meadows cattle dates: MEDIUM (seasonally consistent but exact annual dates vary)
- Lass O'Richmond Hill dog-inside confirmation: MEDIUM
- White Hart Twickenham dog access: MEDIUM
- Hammerton's Ferry seasonal operation: MEDIUM (check current season dates each year)

### A3. Richmond to Kew

**Distance**
Richmond Bridge to Kew Bridge: approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) one way along the south (Surrey) bank via the Thames Path.

**The Riverside Walk to Kew: Can You Walk Without Paying Kew Gardens Entry?**
Yes. The Thames Path between Richmond and Kew runs along the outside of the Kew Gardens wall for a significant section but does not require entry into the Royal Botanic Gardens. The riverside towpath passes the Brentford Gate/Isleworth bank and the southern bank path continues to Kew Bridge. You walk along the Thames without entering the gardens. Kew Gardens does have a riverside walk inside the gardens but that requires full admission.

**Kew Green**
Kew Green is the village green in the centre of Kew, adjacent to the main gates of the Royal Botanic Gardens. It is a public open space (not part of the RBG). Dogs are welcome on Kew Green and it is used by local dog walkers. The green is not fenced and opens onto the surrounding streets.

**Richmond to Kew: Route Character**
From Richmond Bridge, the south bank path passes through the area below Richmond Hill, with Petersham Meadows on the right (see A2 — cattle seasonally, dogs on leads when grazed). Past Petersham the path becomes more suburban, passing through the area near Isleworth and the entrance to Syon Park. The path is a mix of tarmac towpath and compacted paths. It is generally flat throughout.

**Syon Park adjacency**: The Thames Path passes below Syon Park. Dogs are welcome in Syon Park (the grounds, not the house or garden centre) but this is a short detour off the main river path.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs: Richmond to Kew**

The Waterman's Arms, Brentford (near Kew Bridge): Address: 10 Broadway, Brentford, TW8 0LS. Confirmed dog-friendly pub near Kew Bridge at the Brentford end. A relaxed local pub. Dogs welcome inside.

The City Barge, Strand on the Green (Chiswick, just past Kew Bridge): Note — technically this is in the A4 section (Chiswick to Hammersmith) but it is immediately accessible once you cross Kew Bridge going north. This is one of the most famous riverside pubs on the Thames (see A4 for detail).

The Tap on the Line, Kew Gardens Station: Not directly on the towpath but within 5 minutes of it. A Wetherspoons-owned pub in a former railway building. Dog access variable (large Wetherspoons typically permits dogs).

**CONFIDENCE: A3**
- Public access along Kew riverside without RBG entry: HIGH
- Waterman's Arms dog access: MEDIUM
- Kew Green dog access: HIGH (public open space)

### A4. Chiswick to Hammersmith

**Distance and Surface**
Chiswick (Strand on the Green, near Kew Bridge) to Hammersmith Bridge: approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) one way along the north (Middlesex) bank via the Thames Path/riverside path. Surface is predominantly tarmac and compacted paths along this urban/semi-urban stretch.

**Strand on the Green**
Strand on the Green is a charming riverside terrace of 17th and 18th century cottages and houses, immediately downstream (east) from Kew Bridge on the north (Chiswick/Middlesex) bank. The riverside path here is narrow (a raised footway in places) and very scenic. This is one of the most distinctive short stretches of Thames-side walking in London — it feels like a country village riverside but is in zone 3. Dogs very welcome on this stretch and it is a popular dog-walking corridor.

**Chiswick Eyot**
Chiswick Eyot is a small uninhabited island (an 'eyot' is a Thames island) in the Thames between Strand on the Green and Chiswick. It is owned by the Port of London Authority and is a designated Local Nature Reserve and Site of Metropolitan Importance for Nature Conservation. It is used as a nesting site for birds including reed warblers and other wetland birds.

Can dogs access Chiswick Eyot? The island is only accessible on foot at very low tides (it can be reached across mud/shingle at extreme low water). Public access is not formally granted and the island is not signposted as a visitor destination. In practice, some dog walkers do wade/walk across at extreme low tide but this is informal and potentially discouraged given the bird nesting status. Seasonal bird nesting restrictions: the primary nesting season is April to August and access during this period would be particularly problematic for ground-nesting species. The island is best appreciated from the south bank or from passing boats rather than visited with dogs.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs on this Stretch**

The City Barge: Address: 27 Strand on the Green, Chiswick, W4 3PH. One of the most famous dog-friendly Thames pubs. A historic pub (Grade II listed building) directly on the Strand on the Green riverfront. Dogs confirmed welcome inside the bar area. Outside seating directly on the river. A reliable, highly recommended stop.

The Bull's Head, Strand on the Green: Address: 15 Strand on the Green, W4 3PQ. Another pub on Strand on the Green. Dogs confirmed welcome. This is a Young's pub. Outside seating area. Dogs permitted in the bar.

The Mawson Arms: Address: 110 Chiswick Lane South, W4 2QA (this is technically just off the main river path, near Fuller's Griffin Brewery). This is the brewery tap for Fuller's London Pride — the only pub where you can drink on the brewery site. Dogs confirmed welcome. It is a short detour off the Thames Path but highly distinctive for beer enthusiasts. Note: it has limited opening hours compared to a standard pub (typically Mon–Fri only in some periods).

The Dove, Hammersmith: Address: 19 Upper Mall, Hammersmith, W6 9TA. Just before Hammersmith Bridge. One of the most famous Thames pubs in London. Dogs confirmed welcome in the bar and riverside terrace. This is a historic pub (17th century) with a very small riverside terrace and a cosy bar. Consistently rated highly for dog access.

**What Makes This Stretch Distinctive**
The combination of Strand on the Green (village character), Fuller's Brewery adjacency, Chiswick Mall (another stretch of historic riverside houses), and the string of genuinely historic pubs makes this one of the most rewarding urban Thames walks in London. It connects naturally with the Richmond/Kew walks above or with central London walks below.

- City Barge dog inside: HIGH (widely confirmed)
- Bull's Head dog access: MEDIUM
- Mawson Arms opening hours: MEDIUM (verify current hours before visit)
- The Dove dog access: HIGH (widely confirmed)

### A5. Pangbourne to Goring (Goring Gap)

**Overview**
The Goring Gap is the point where the Thames cuts through the Chiltern Hills and the North Wessex Downs, creating an unusually dramatic river valley landscape by Thames standards. The walk between Pangbourne (Berkshire) and Goring (Oxfordshire) is one of the most celebrated sections of the Thames Path in the middle Thames.

**Distance and Walking Time**
Pangbourne to Goring: approximately 6 miles (9.6 km) one way along the Thames Path. Walking time approximately 2.5–3 hours at a relaxed pace. Total out-and-back is 12 miles. The linear walk is most practical using the Great Western Railway line which connects both Pangbourne and Goring & Streatley stations on the Reading–Didcot line.

**Start Points**
- Pangbourne station: RG8 7AH (nearby postcode). Small car park at the station; additional parking in Pangbourne village.
- Goring & Streatley station: RG8 9HH. The station is in Goring, a short walk from the river.

**Stile-Free Confirmation**
The Thames Path in this section is generally considered one of the more accessible sections of the National Trail. Most gates are kissing gates or wide gates rather than stiles. However, full stile-free confirmation for the Pangbourne–Goring stretch is MEDIUM confidence — the Thames Path Association regularly updates this and some kissing gates may be a constraint for very large dogs. The National Trails accessibility guidance notes this section as mostly accessible. Current diversions: this section has been subject to periodic path diversions particularly at Gatehampton Farm — the path was diverted from the riverside through the farmyard at points. As of available 2025 reporting this section is open but the riverside path adjacent to Basildon Park has been variable. CHECK current Thames Path official website before visiting.

**Cattle and Livestock Sections**
The most significant livestock section is through the Hartslock area and the fields approaching Goring. Between Pangbourne and Goring, the path passes through several privately farmed fields where cattle may be present seasonally. The stretch at Gatehampton (approximately halfway between Pangbourne and Goring) historically has had cattle. Specific seasonal dates for cattle in these fields are not consistently published. The general guidance is: spring to autumn (April–October) is when cattle are most likely present in riverside fields on this section. When cattle are present, keep dogs on leads.

Hartslock Nature Reserve (BBOWT managed, between Pangbourne and Goring on the south bank): This is a significant chalk grassland reserve known for rare orchids (including monkey orchids). Dogs are permitted on the footpath through the reserve but must be kept on leads and strictly on the path during the orchid season (spring). The reserve is very small but the path passes through it.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs**

In Pangbourne:
The Cross Keys: Confirmed dog-friendly in the bar. A village pub in Pangbourne close to the river.
The Elephant Hotel/pub: Traditional village pub in Pangbourne; dog access reports are mixed — verify.

In Goring & Streatley:
The Miller of Mansfield, Goring: Address: High Street, Goring-on-Thames, RG8 9AW. A gastro-pub/restaurant with rooms. Dogs reportedly welcome in the bar area. Confirm before visiting as it has shifted between allowing and not allowing dogs in recent years.
The Catherine Wheel, Goring: A more traditional pub. Dog access: MEDIUM confidence.
The Bull at Streatley: Address: High Street, Streatley, RG8 9JJ. Dogs welcome in bar confirmed from multiple sources.

**Train Connection**
Great Western Railway, Reading–Didcot line. Trains connect Pangbourne and Goring & Streatley stations. Journey time approximately 10–15 minutes. Service is approximately 2 trains per hour during the day. This makes the linear walk straightforward.

**CONFIDENCE: A5**
- Stile-free: MEDIUM (mostly yes, check current trail reports)
- Cattle locations and months: MEDIUM (seasonal but exact annual placement varies)
- Path diversions at Gatehampton: MEDIUM (check Thames Path website before visit)
- Miller of Mansfield dog policy: LOW-MEDIUM (variable reports)

### A6. Cookham to Marlow

**Overview**
One of the most diverse single-day Thames walks. Combines Stanley Spencer country (Cookham), National Trust open land (Cock Marsh), extraordinary river views, and a pub accessible only by boat or on foot (The Bounty), finishing in the attractive town of Marlow.

**Distance**
Cookham to Marlow: approximately 5–6 miles (8–9.6 km) via the Thames Path south bank route depending on exact starting point and route variations. A standard day walk with train return.

**Start Point**
Cookham station (SL6 9BN nearby). There is also parking in Cookham village (Cookham Moor car park, SL6 9QS, free National Trust car park on the common — MEDIUM confidence on postcode, verify on NT website).

**Cock Marsh**
Cock Marsh is a National Trust owned area of unenclosed common land on the south (Berkshire) bank of the Thames between Cookham and Bourne End. It is ancient common land and is open access under National Trust management. Dogs are confirmed welcome on Cock Marsh. It is effectively open common land — no exclusion zones. There is no livestock typically on Cock Marsh itself (it is managed differently from grazed meadows). Dogs can be off-lead on Cock Marsh throughout the year. The National Trust does not impose lead requirements here in normal circumstances. The marsh includes a mixture of grassland, rough scrub, and views towards Winter Hill (above). A genuine highlight of this walk.

**Winter Hill**
Winter Hill is the high ground above Cock Marsh, offering panoramic views over the Thames Valley. It is accessible by a short climb from Cock Marsh. The climb is steep but short. Dogs easily managed on lead or off-lead depending on your dog. The view is genuinely spectacular — one of the best viewpoints on this section of the Thames.

**The Bounty**
The Bounty is a pub on the south bank of the Thames at Bourne End, between Cookham and Marlow. It is only accessible by walking the Thames Path or arriving by boat — there is no road access. This makes it one of the most distinctive pub stops on the whole Thames. Dogs are confirmed welcome at The Bounty — it is a consistently dog-friendly establishment that is used to the walk-in (or row-in) clientele. Outdoor seating directly on the riverbank. This pub is a genuine highlight of the Cookham–Marlow walk.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs in Marlow**
The Compleat Angler (Macdonald hotel): Address: Marlow Bridge, SL7 1RG. Dogs welcome in some areas — this is a hotel/dining establishment; dog access to bar is MEDIUM confidence. More of a smart dining venue.
The Two Brewers, Marlow: A town centre pub. Dog access MEDIUM confidence.
The Crown Hotel Marlow: SL7 2JP. Dogs reportedly welcome in the bar.
Verify current dog policies in Marlow as it is a prosperous Buckinghamshire town with a range of options.

**Return Options from Marlow**
Marlow is on the Great Western Railway line (Maidenhead to Bourne End and Marlow branch line). Trains run to Maidenhead, connecting to the Elizabeth line/Crossrail for London. The line terminates at Marlow (SL7 2RH nearby). Journey time Marlow to Maidenhead approximately 20 minutes, then Maidenhead to Paddington approximately 25–30 minutes.
Bus: Thames Valley bus services connect Marlow to High Wycombe and surrounding areas.

**CONFIDENCE: A6**
- Cock Marsh off-lead and NT open access: HIGH
- The Bounty dog access: HIGH (widely confirmed)
- Winter Hill viewpoint: HIGH
- Marlow pub dog access specifics: MEDIUM (verify before visiting)
- Cookham Moor NT car park postcode: MEDIUM (verify on NT website)

### A7. Henley to Hambleden Mill

**Overview**
A quiet, beautiful section of the upper Thames past Temple Island, along the flat Berkshire meadows, and up to the picturesque Hambleden Mill at the end of Hambleden Lock. This is an underrated walk, significantly less known than the Goring Gap or the Cookham–Marlow section.

**Distance**
Henley-on-Thames to Hambleden Mill (via Thames Path south bank): approximately 4–5 miles (6.4–8 km) one way depending on start point and whether you continue into Hambleden village. Out-and-back is 8–10 miles or linear with return from Hambleden.

**Start Point**
Henley-on-Thames. Nearest station: Henley-on-Thames (branch line from Twyford on GWR). Station postcode area: RG9 2LQ. Town centre car parks including Mill Meadows car park (RG9 1BF, pay and display, riverside location adjacent to the start of the walk).

**Temple Island Meadows**
Shortly after leaving Henley on the Thames Path (proceeding west/upstream, so actually heading north-west following the river), the path passes Temple Island. Temple Island is the official start point of the Henley Royal Regatta course. It sits in the middle of the river and is not accessible from the bank but is visible from the path on both banks. The meadows between Henley and Temple Island are open grassland. The Thames Path here is flat and follows the river closely. Dogs and the wide open meadows are well-matched.

**Hambleden Mill**
Hambleden Mill (also called the Mill at Hambleden or Hambleden Weir) is at Hambleden Lock, where a very photogenic white-painted former flour mill sits beside the weir and lock. It was converted to luxury apartments but the exterior and weir area remain accessible to walkers. The lock itself and towpath around it are publicly accessible and free. Dogs can visit the area. There is no entry fee or restriction for walkers. This is one of the most photographed spots on the upper Thames.

**Hambleden Village**
Hambleden village is a short walk inland from the mill (approximately 1 mile up the valley road/footpath). It is a picture-postcard Chiltern flint-and-brick village. The pub in the village: The Stag and Huntsman, Hambleden (RG9 6RP). Dogs confirmed welcome at The Stag and Huntsman — it is a traditional village pub and well-known for being dog-friendly. Dogs are permitted inside the bar. Water bowls available. This is a genuine recommendation.

**Dog-Friendly Pubs Between Henley and Hambleden**
On the route itself (Thames Path bank), there are limited pub options between Henley and Hambleden as this is a rural section. The main pub stops are:
- Henley itself has numerous dog-friendly pubs. The Angel on the Bridge (RG9 1BH) is directly at Henley Bridge on the river — dogs in bar confirmed. The Little Angel (Remenham Lane, RG9 3DB) is immediately across the bridge — dogs welcome.
- Aston village (slightly north of the direct route): The Flower Pot Hotel, Aston (RG9 3DG) — confirmed dog-friendly, well-known for dogs inside and in the garden.

**Return Options from Hambleden**
The walk is generally done as an out-and-back, returning to Henley along the same Thames Path route. There is no train station at Hambleden or the immediately surrounding villages. Bus services from Henley connect to surrounding areas but are infrequent. If extending the walk, the Thames Path continues upstream from Hambleden Lock towards Medmenham and eventually Marlow (approximately a further 5 miles) where there is a train station.

**CONFIDENCE: A7**

- Stag and Huntsman dog access: HIGH (widely confirmed)
- Flower Pot Hotel Aston dog access: HIGH
- Angel on the Bridge dog access: MEDIUM
- Temple Island access from bank: HIGH (viewable only, not accessible)

### A8. Oxford — Port Meadow

**Overview**
Port Meadow is one of England's most remarkable urban open spaces: a large, flat, unimproved (never ploughed in recorded history) floodplain meadow immediately west of Oxford, flooding in winter and grazed in warmer months. It is common land with Freemen of Oxford rights and is owned by Oxford City Council. It is a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI).

**Size**
Port Meadow is approximately 440 acres (178 hectares). Combined with adjoining Wolvercote Common to the north, the area extends to approximately 300 hectares.

**Cattle and Horses: Year-Round Presence**
Port Meadow has cattle and horses grazing on it throughout much of the year, though the pattern varies. Historically the Freemen of Oxford have the right to graze animals on Port Meadow. Horses are present for much of the year. Cattle are typically present from late spring through autumn. The meadow can be completely flooded in winter (November to February) and grazing animals are removed during flood periods. During summer and autumn, livestock (both horses and cattle) are reliably present. Dogs MUST be kept under close control (and ideally on a lead) around the livestock — horses in particular can be reactive to dogs. This is a legal requirement on common land with grazing animals.

It is not a simple year-round off-lead destination — off-lead is workable in winter when flooded (but the path conditions are poor) and in early spring before cattle/horses arrive, or in very specific sections away from the grazing animals.

**Parking Options**
- Cripley Road, Oxford: OX1 1QJ (approximate — near the railway station/Oxpens Road area, walking access to Port Meadow from the south). Not a formal car park for Port Meadow directly.

### B3. Kennet and Avon Canal — Devizes to Bradford on Avon

**Overview**
This is arguably the finest day-walk section of the entire Kennet and Avon Canal. It passes the remarkable Caen Hill Locks flight, through the open Wiltshire countryside, to the beautiful town of Bradford on Avon. Total distance approximately 15 miles (24 km) one way — this is a full day walk or a comfortable two-day section. The best single-day dog walk on this section is probably the shorter Bradford on Avon to Devizes (or Honeystreet) section.

**Distance: Devizes to Bradford on Avon (full section)**
Approximately 14–15 miles (22–24 km) one way. For a single day walk, Bradford on Avon to Pewsey (via Honeystreet) is approximately 10–12 miles; or Bradford on Avon to Devizes (the Caen Hill section specifically) is approximately 9 miles.

**Caen Hill Locks**
Caen Hill Locks is a flight of 29 locks on the Kennet and Avon Canal near Devizes. It is the longest continuous flight of locks in the UK (the wider Devizes flight including flanking locks is 29 in total). The locks are a nationally significant canal heritage site and managed by Canal & River Trust. Dogs can visit the Caen Hill Locks — the towpath runs alongside all locks. It is a spectacular engineering sight. The area around the top of the flight (Devizes end) has a CaRT visitor facility and cafe (the Caen Hill Cafe — dogs welcome outside). Dog waste bins are present at the locks area. This is one of the most distinctive sights on any English canal towpath.

**The Barge Inn at Honeystreet**
Address: Honeystreet, Pewsey, Wiltshire, SN9 5PS. The Barge Inn is a famous canal-side pub in the remote Vale of Pewsey, equidistant from Devizes and Bradford on Avon (more or less). It is set in an absolutely isolated stretch of the canal with fields and Wiltshire downland on all sides. Dogs are confirmed welcome — this is a well-established dog-friendly pub with a large canal-side outdoor area and camping. The Barge Inn is also famous for being a centre for crop circle enthusiasts (the Vale of Pewsey is a noted crop circle area). Dogs welcome inside the pub. A genuinely distinctive and memorable canal-side stop.

**Bradford on Avon: The Lock Inn**
Address: 48 Frome Road, Bradford on Avon, BA15 1LE (near the Bradford on Avon canal wharf/lock). The Lock Inn is a canal-side cafe/pub directly on the canal at Bradford on Avon. Dogs are confirmed welcome — dogs inside and on the canal-side terrace. This is the quintessential end-of-walk refreshment stop on this route. Cycle hire also available here. Highly recommended for dogs.

**The Canal Tavern, Bradford on Avon**
Address: 17 Canal Road, Bradford on Avon, BA15 1LS. A pub near the canal in Bradford on Avon. Dog access MEDIUM confidence — verify before visiting.

**Seasonal Restrictions: Kennet and Avon**
No seasonal walking restrictions on this section of the K&A Canal towpath. The towpath can be muddy in winter (Wiltshire clay sections are challenging after rain). Cattle may be present in fields adjacent to the towpath in some sections between Devizes and Bradford on Avon — keep dogs on leads if livestock adjacent to path.

### C1. Canbury Gardens and Ham Lands Circular (Kingston)

*(This section expands on A1 with additional circular-specific detail)*

**Ham Lands Land Management**
Ham Lands is a Local Nature Reserve (LNR) managed by the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames. It is NOT a London Wildlife Trust (LWT) reserve — LWT manages different sites in London. LB Richmond manages Ham Lands primarily for biodiversity (chalk grassland restoration, wildflower meadows, scrub management). There is no livestock on Ham Lands.

**Off-Lead Status on Ham Lands**
Officially, LB Richmond does not designate Ham Lands as an off-lead zone. However, there are no formal dogs-on-leads requirements posted at Ham Lands in normal circumstances. In practice, Ham Lands is used as an off-lead area by local dog walkers throughout the year. The LB Richmond signage typically asks for considerate dog walking and requests leads near specific sensitive areas (if any ground-nesting bird management is active in spring). This is one of those areas where practice significantly exceeds formal designation.

**Full Circular Distance**
Kingston Bridge → south bank to Teddington Lock → cross to Middlesex bank → Ham Lands → south along Ham riverside path → Richmond Bridge → cross to Surrey bank → south bank to Kingston Bridge: approximately 9–10 miles.

Shorter version (Ham Lands detour and return): Kingston Bridge south bank to Teddington Lock (3.5 miles) → cross to Ham Lands and walk to Ham House area and back to Teddington Lock (approximately 2 miles detour) → return south bank to Kingston Bridge (3.5 miles) = approximately 9 miles out-and-back with Ham Lands detour.

**The Footbridge at Teddington Lock**
At Teddington Lock there are in fact two footbridges:
1. The lock bridge (concrete footbridge over the lock chamber itself)
2. The footbridge over the weir/lasher section

Both are free, open to the public, and cross from the Surrey bank to the Middlesex bank. They connect at the Teddington Lock island/complex. This is the specific crossing point to access Ham Lands from Kingston side.

**Kingston Bridge to Teddington Lock South Bank Distance**
Approximately 3.5 miles (5.6 km).

**Return via Ham Side Route**
From the Teddington Lock footbridge on the Middlesex bank, heading south (towards Ham and Richmond) through Ham Lands, the route follows the Thames bank path. The path south of Ham Lands passes Ham Street, then along the riverfront past Ham House grounds, then continues to Petersham/Richmond. The Ham bank path is generally well-defined and pleasant — a mixture of open riverside, scrub, and the formal grounds approach to Ham House.

- Canbury Gardens: public toilets present (at the south end near the car park, confirmed RBK facility)

**Overview**
Oxleas Wood, Shepherdleas Wood, Oxleas Meadow, and Jack Wood together form one of the largest areas of ancient woodland in Greater London, and one of the oldest continuously wooded sites in England (ancient woodland of 8,000+ years continuous existence). Together with Shooters Hill above, this is a genuinely remarkable and significantly underrated dog walking destination for a southeast London location.

**Size**
The Oxleas complex (Oxleas Wood, Shepherdleas Wood, Jack Wood, and Oxleas Meadow) is approximately 133 hectares confirmed. This figure is well established in the site documentation from the London Borough of Greenwich and the Woodland Trust who helped campaign to protect it (the M3 motorway extension was proposed through it in the 1990s and blocked by public campaign).

**Severndroog Castle**
Severndroog Castle is an 18th century triangular gothic castle folly on the summit of Shooters Hill (the highest point in SE London at approximately 130m above sea level). It was built in 1784 by Lady Anne James in memory of her husband Sir William James. It is a Grade II listed building and has been restored by the Severndroog Castle Building Preservation Trust.

Dogs at Severndroog Castle: The castle itself (exterior) is freely accessible — it sits in the castle wood/grounds. Dogs can walk to the castle and around the exterior. The interior of the castle has been restored and opened to visitors; the standard NT/heritage site rule of dogs on leads near the building applies. The grounds of Shooters Hill woods around the castle are fully dog-walkable.

**Cafe in the Woods: Oxleas Wood Cafe**
The Oxleas Wood Cafe is a well-established facility in Oxleas Meadow, managed by Greenwich Leisure Limited (GLL). Dogs are welcome outside in the outdoor seating area. There are reports of dogs being permitted in the covered porch/veranda area but inside the main building is dogs-on-request basis. This is the most commonly referenced facilities point for the Oxleas walk. Water bowls are available outside the cafe.

**Off-Lead**
The entire Oxleas complex (all the woodland areas and Oxleas Meadow) is dog-friendly and off-lead throughout. There are no livestock, no formal dog exclusion zones, and no on-lead requirements in the woodland and meadow sections. This is confirmed by LB Greenwich and is one of the significant advantages of this site.

**Parking**
The Oxleas Wood/Shooters Hill area parking: The main car park used by visitors is the Oxleas Wood Car Park (also called the Castle Wood car park or Shooters Hill car park) on Shooters Hill Road (A207). Postcode: SE18 3JA is the commonly cited postcode for the Oxleas Wood/Shooters Hill area. There is also parking at Eltham Park approach roads.

**What Makes It Genuinely Distinctive**
1. Ancient woodland (Neolithic to present — unbroken woodland cover for approximately 8,000 years). This is exceptionally rare in Greater London.
2. The highest viewpoint in SE London.
3. The juxtaposition of a gothic folly (Severndroog Castle) with ancient woodland.
4. Only 30 minutes from central London by rail and completely un-touristy.
5. The cafe in the woods format is genuinely pleasant.
6. Off-lead throughout a large area.

### C3. Holkham Beach and Pinewoods (Norfolk)

**Overview**
Holkham Beach and the surrounding Holkham National Nature Reserve is one of England's finest coastal landscapes: vast, flat sand, pine plantations (the pinewoods), saltmarsh, and an almost complete absence of the facilities of busier tourist beaches. It is managed by Holkham Estate (the de Grey family).

**Dog Restrictions: Specific Zones and Seasonal Dates**
This is one of the most important points for this walk. Holkham NNR has a seasonal dog access scheme:

The main beach (the open sand between the dunes and the sea): Dogs are NOT permitted on sections of Holkham Beach during the summer nesting season. The specific restriction applies to the western end of the beach (Beach Road to the west) from 1 April to 31 August. This is a ground-nesting bird protection zone for lapwing, ringed plover, and oystercatcher. During this period, dog access on the main beach is restricted to the section in front of Lady Anne's Drive (the central access road) only.

Important: the exact zone boundaries change slightly from year to year based on where ground-nesting birds are located. Holkham Estate puts up marker posts indicating the restricted zones each spring. The restriction is legally enforced under nature reserve management powers.

Off-season (1 September to 31 March): Dogs are permitted on all sections of Holkham Beach, including the western end and the eastern section towards Wells-next-the-Sea.

**The Pinewoods: Year-Round Dog Access**
The Holkham pinewoods (the Corsican pine plantation behind the dunes) are accessible to dogs year-round. There are no seasonal dog restrictions in the pinewoods themselves. This is an important distinction — even during the April–August beach restriction period, walkers with dogs can use the pinewoods path parallel to the beach. The pinewood path runs between Lady Anne's Drive and the dunes. Dogs on leads or under close control in the pinewoods is requested (deer are present).

**Parking: Lady Anne's Drive**
Lady Anne's Drive is the main access road to Holkham Beach from the A149. Parking postcode: NR23 1RG is the commonly cited postcode for the Lady Anne's Drive car park (pay and display, managed by Holkham Estate). The car park is large and opens early (typically 7am or 8am). Busy in summer, particularly weekends.

**Distance of a Good Walk**
Lady Anne's Drive car park to the beach, along the pinewoods path, then east along the beach to Wells-next-the-Sea, returning via the Wells-Holkham route: approximately 5–6 miles circular, depending on how far east you walk. A simpler out-and-back from Lady Anne's Drive to the beach and back through the pinewoods is 2–3 miles.

**The Victoria at Holkham**
Address: Park Road, Holkham, Wells-next-the-Sea, NR23 1RG. The Victoria is the Holkham Estate's hotel and pub, immediately adjacent to the Lady Anne's Drive car park. Dogs are confirmed welcome at The Victoria — dogs are welcome in the bar area. This is consistently confirmed as dog-friendly. The Victoria is a quality pub/hotel with good food. Dogs in the bar: HIGH confidence.

### C4. Sandwich Bay (Kent)

**What is Here**
Sandwich Bay is a Site of Special Scientific Interest on the Kent coast, between Sandwich town (on the River Stour) and Deal. It is characterised by:
- Sand dunes (Sandwich Bay dune system, one of the finest in southeast England)
- Shingle beach
- Sandwich Bay Bird Observatory (important migration watchpoint)
- Stour Valley nature reserve
- The open beach stretching south towards Deal

It is reached from Sandwich via the Sandwich Bay Road (the private toll road — there is a small toll charge to drive through to the beach, approximately £4–5 per vehicle, payable at the toll booth). Alternatively, the area can be reached on foot from Sandwich town (approximately 2 miles walk).

**Dog Access: Year-Round**
There are no formal seasonal dog bans on the main beach or dune system at Sandwich Bay, unlike many more intensively managed beaches. The beach and dunes are accessible year-round with dogs. The dune slack areas and nature reserve sections (particularly the Stour valley and the areas behind the dunes managed by Kent Wildlife Trust) may have seasonal guidance about dogs on leads during nesting periods (spring/early summer) but these are not uniformly enforced exclusions.

**What Makes It Distinctive and Underrated**
1. The toll road creates a barrier to casual visitors — Sandwich Bay genuinely quiet compared to other Kent beaches.
2. The dune system is one of the best in SE England.
3. Sandwich town (medieval Cinque Port) is directly accessible from the beach via the Stour path.
4. Rich birdwatching (migration season) combined with uncrowded beach walks.
5. Almost completely undeveloped coastline.
6. Connection to Royal St George's Golf Club (major championship venue — the links are immediately adjacent).

**Distance of a Good Walk**
Sandwich town → Sandwich Bay (via Stour path and dunes) → south along beach towards Deal → return via dune path: approximately 6–8 miles circular depending on beach distance chosen.
Or: from the Sandwich Bay toll road car park → north along beach to Pegwell Bay → return: approximately 4–5 miles out-and-back.

**Parking and Access**
The toll road car park near the beach: CT13 9PH (Sandwich Bay area postcode). Pay the toll to drive to the beach end. Alternatively, park in Sandwich town (CT13 9JF town centre) and walk out.

- No formal seasonal dog ban on main beach: MEDIUM-HIGH (unlike many managed beaches, but verify current year restrictions as these can change)

- Dune and shingle character: HIGH

### C5. Mersea Island (Essex)

**Overview**
Mersea Island is a small island off the Essex coast, connected to the mainland by The Strood, a tidal causeway. It combines a genuine tidal island experience, excellent dog walking on the foreshore and at Cudmore Grove Country Park, and a strong local identity centred on oysters and seafood.

**The Strood: Tidal Causeway**
The Strood is the only road connection between Mersea Island and the mainland. It floods at high tide. The flooding is predictable by tide table and happens approximately twice daily. When The Strood floods (typically for 1–3 hours around high tide depending on the height of the tide and wind conditions), the island is genuinely cut off.

How often does it flood? On a typical spring tide (the highest tides, occurring around new and full moon) the Strood floods approximately twice a week for periods of up to 2–3 hours at a time. On neap tides (lowest high tides) it may not flood at all or only briefly. The frequency varies significantly with the lunar cycle and weather conditions (strong southerly winds and storm surges can extend flooding significantly).

Tide table resource: Mersea Island residents use the Mersea Island website (mersea.co.uk) which publishes a Strood tide table. Also, Mersea Island Parish Council provides Strood crossing information. The tide times for Mersea are close to Harwich tidal predictions (available from the UKHO/Easytide website).

**Cudmore Grove Country Park**
Address: Bromans Lane, East Mersea, CO5 8UE. Managed by Essex County Council. Dogs are confirmed welcome at Cudmore Grove Country Park. The park includes a beach (foreshore), grassland, and woodland. Dogs are permitted throughout the park and beach. Off-lead areas are available — the beach foreshore is used for off-lead dog exercise. Dog waste bins confirmed at the car park and main access points. The beach at Cudmore Grove gives views across the estuary to the Colne and Blackwater confluence.

**West Mersea Dog-Friendly Pubs and Seafood**
The Company Shed: Address: 129 Coast Road, West Mersea, CO5 8PA. This is one of the best-known seafood sheds in Essex — locally caught oysters, crab, fish. It is a very basic counter-service setup. Dogs are welcome outside on the quayside. This is more a seafood shack than a pub but it is the most famous food destination on the island and dogs are accommodated outside.

The Coast Inn: Address: 1 Coast Road, West Mersea, CO5 8LS. A pub on the West Mersea waterfront. Dogs confirmed welcome. A traditional seaside pub.

The Victory: Address: 1 Coast Road, West Mersea, CO5 8LS area (Victory and Coast Inn are close together on the waterfront). Dog access: MEDIUM confidence.

**Distance of a Good Circular**
Cudmore Grove Country Park → foreshore path → along East Mersea foreshore → towards West Mersea → return via inland field paths: approximately 5–7 miles depending on route choices.
West Mersea foreshore to Cudmore Grove via the coastal path: approximately 3 miles one way.

**What Makes It Unusual**
1. The genuine tidal island experience — checking the Strood tide times before visiting is part of the trip.
2. Outstanding flat marshland and estuary scenery.
3. The combination of country park, beach, and excellent local seafood in a compact area.
4. Significantly less visited than better-known Essex coastal points.
5. Mersea oysters are among the most famous in England — combining a great dog walk with some of the best native oysters and crab in the country.

**CONFIDENCE: C5**
- The Strood flooding frequency: HIGH (tidal, well documented)
- Cudmore Grove dog access: HIGH
- The Company Shed dogs outside: HIGH
- The Coast Inn dog access: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Distance: MEDIUM

### C6. Epping Forest — Best Specific Routes

**Overview**
Epping Forest is a Royal Forest of approximately 2,400 hectares (6,000 acres) stretching from Chingford in the south to Epping in the north, managed by the City of London Corporation. It is a unique combination of ancient pollarded beech and hornbeam woodland, heath, and grassland. Dogs are welcome throughout Epping Forest with no formal lead requirements in most areas.

**Route 1: Connaught Water Circuit from Chingford**
Parking: The Warren car park (Bury Road), Chingford, E4 7QJ is the principal parking area adjacent to Connaught Water. Alternatively, the Queen Elizabeth Hunting Lodge car park on Rangers Road, E4 7QH is very close and has The Butler's Retreat cafe adjacent.

Connaught Water: An ornamental lake in Epping Forest, created in 1880s. It has waterfowl (mallards, coots, cormorants, Canada geese). Dogs can walk around the lake on the surrounding paths. Dogs may paddle at the water's edge in accessible sections. Bird restriction: There is no formal seasonal dog ban at Connaught Water, but some sections of the reed margins are used by nesting birds in spring/summer. Keep dogs under control near reed beds April–July.

Route: Connaught Water circuit (approximately 1.5 miles just for the water circuit) extended into the adjacent forest (towards Fairmead or High Beach): 3–5 miles depending on extension.

The Butler's Retreat Cafe: Address: Rangers Road, Chingford, E4 7QH. A long-established cafe at the edge of Epping Forest near the Hunting Lodge. Dogs welcome outside in the seating area. Not dogs-inside confirmed.

Queen Elizabeth's Hunting Lodge: The actual lodge building. A museum managed by the City of London. Dogs outside only in the grounds.

**Route 2: High Beach and Baldwins Hill Circular**
High Beach is a hamlet in the heart of Epping Forest. Parking: High Beach car park (Kings Oak car park area), IG10 4AE. The Kings Oak pub is immediately adjacent to this car park.

Kings Oak Pub: Address: High Beach, Loughton, IG10 4AF. Confirmed dog-friendly — this is one of the most popular post-walk pubs in Epping Forest. Dogs in the garden. Reports of dogs in bar area. This is a large pub with a big outdoor area.

Baldwins Hill: The area around Baldwins Hill, Loughton, gives access to some of the best ancient pollarded beech woods in the forest. Circular route via High Beach, Baldwins Hill, and the valley below: approximately 4–5 miles. Entirely off-lead in the woodland.

**Route 3: Theydon Bois to Epping via the Epping Forest**
Theydon Bois is at the south end of the Central Line (tube). Epping is at the north terminus. Walking between the two through the forest gives a genuine feeling of wilderness despite being Zone 6.

Start: Theydon Bois tube station, CM16 4LP vicinity.
End: Epping tube station, CM16 4HS vicinity.
Distance: approximately 5–6 miles through the forest, not retracing steps.
Off-lead: entirely off-lead throughout the forest sections.
Return: Take the tube from Epping back to Theydon Bois (2 stops on Central Line) — dogs travel free on the tube if on a lead or in a carrier.
Notable feature: this linear walk uses the forest's full north-south depth and is almost entirely within the forest. Very limited facilities mid-route.

**Off-Lead Status, Epping Forest Generally**
The City of London Corporation's management of Epping Forest does not formally require dogs on leads except in specific areas (such as near livestock grazing enclosures — the Corporation does graze cattle in some sections, notably around the Warren section near Loughton). In the main forest, dogs are off-lead throughout.

### C7. Delamere Forest (Cheshire)

**Overview**
Delamere Forest is Cheshire's largest area of woodland (approximately 972 hectares), managed by Forestry England. It combines conifer plantation and native woodland with good walking and cycling trails. It is significantly underrated as a dog destination and highly practical for the northwest England population.

**Main Car Park and Access**
The main visitor car park is the Delamere Forest Visitor Centre car park, Station Road, Delamere, Northwich, CW8 2JD. This is immediately adjacent to Delamere Forest railway station (served by Northern Rail Crewe–Chester line), making it one of the most rail-accessible Forestry England sites in the country.

**Blakemere Trail Distance**
The Blakemere Trail is the main trail at Delamere, looping around Blakemere Moss (a large shallow lake/wetland in the heart of the forest). The Blakemere Trail is approximately 5.5 miles (8.8 km). It is waymarked, well-surfaced on most sections, and clearly the most popular route in the forest.

**Off-Lead Confirmation**
Forestry England confirms that dogs are welcome at Delamere Forest throughout the publicly accessible areas and trails. There are no formal lead-only requirements in the forest itself. Dogs can be off-lead in the forest. The exception would be any livestock areas — there is occasional sheep grazing in specific sections for conservation management; look for signage. The Blakemere Trail is effectively a fully off-lead dog walk with no formal restrictions.

**Visitor Centre Cafe: Quinlan's Cafe (Delamere)**
The Forestry England visitor centre has a cafe. Dogs are welcome in the covered outdoor seating/porch area adjacent to the cafe. Dogs are not permitted inside the main cafe building (standard Forestry England policy). Dog water bowls: these are specifically confirmed as being available outside at the Delamere visitor centre cafe — this has been noted in multiple reviews and is a deliberate dog-friendly facility.

**Seasonal Restrictions**
No formal seasonal walking restrictions at Delamere Forest. The forest may have specific closures for timber harvesting operations (standard Forestry England practice — these are temporary and signposted). Bird disturbance areas (particularly around Blakemere Moss which has nesting waterbirds) may have seasonal advisory lead requirements near the water's edge in spring/early summer. These are typically advisory rather than enforced exclusions.

**What is Notable for Dogs Specifically**
1. Blakemere Moss lake shore access — dogs can access the water from specific points along the Blakemere Trail.
2. The size of the forest means dogs can run for hours without roads or hazards.
3. Rail accessibility from Crewe and Chester makes this genuinely accessible without a car.
4. The Delamere terrain is more varied than many Forestry England sites — not just flat uniform plantation.

**CONFIDENCE: C7**
- Main car park postcode CW8 2JD: HIGH
- Blakemere Trail 5.5 miles: HIGH
- Off-lead confirmed: HIGH
- Dog water bowls at visitor centre: MEDIUM-HIGH (multiple reports)
- Rail access from Delamere station: HIGH

### C8. Savernake Forest (Wiltshire)

**Overview**
Savernake Forest near Marlborough, Wiltshire, is one of England's largest and most ancient forests (approximately 4,500 acres / 1,800 hectares of ancient royal forest). It is privately owned (by the Marquess of Ailesbury — the Brudenell-Bruce family/Earl of Cardigan title) but managed under a long lease agreement with Forestry England who manage the public access and much of the planting. It is one of the few private ancient forests in England with full public access confirmed.

**Ownership**
The forest is owned by the Marquess of Ailesbury (the Brudenell-Bruce family). The hereditary Warden title (Earl of Cardigan, held by the eldest son of the Marquess of Ailesbury) is associated with historic wardenship of the forest. The forest is currently managed jointly with Forestry England under a long-term agreement. The Crown Estate previously had involvement historically. The current arrangement gives public open access throughout.

**Grand Avenue**
Grand Avenue is a magnificent straight formal avenue of beech trees running through the heart of Savernake Forest, approximately 4 miles (6.4 km) long. It was created in the 18th century as a processional avenue. It is one of the most impressive tree-lined avenues in England. Dogs can walk the entire length of Grand Avenue — it is a public road/track and also used as a main forest trail.

**Parking: Free**
Savernake Forest has multiple free parking areas. The principal ones:
- Grand Avenue main entrance at the A346 junction: SN8 3PT (approximate — this is the junction area near the forest entrance off the A346 Marlborough–Burbage road). Free roadside/lay-by parking at the forest entrances.
- Forest Hill junction: informal parking areas throughout.
- There is no pay-and-display car park — parking is free throughout. This is one of the great practical advantages of Savernake.

**Off-Lead: Confirmed No Formal Restrictions**
Savernake Forest has no formal dog lead requirements throughout the publicly accessible areas. Dogs can be off-lead throughout the forest. This is consistent with Forestry England management policy.

**Deer and Livestock**
Deer: There is a large deer population in Savernake Forest — fallow deer and muntjac deer are both present. This is the primary reason to have a reliable recall with your dog in Savernake. Dogs chasing deer is both illegal (Wildlife and Countryside Act) and potentially dangerous. The deer are not uniformly distributed and may be encountered anywhere in the forest. This is a genuine hazard/consideration but not a formal restriction.

Livestock: There is no regular livestock grazing in the main forest area. There may be occasional fencing for specific conservation/management purposes but this is not a cattle-grazed forest in the way Port Meadow or Petersham Meadows are.

**What Makes It Genuinely Distinctive vs New Forest**
1. Far less visited than the New Forest — on a weekday you can walk for hours in Savernake and see almost nobody.
2. Free parking (no New Forest pay-and-display systems).
3. The Grand Avenue is architecturally extraordinary — 4 miles of cathedral-beech canopy.
4. Ancient tree population: Savernake has some of the oldest trees in England including the Big Belly Oak, an approximately 1,100-year-old pedunculate oak. Dogs can walk to the Big Belly Oak (on the route from the Grand Avenue junction — look for signage near the main forest crossroads).
5. No formal pay zones, rangers, or visitor centre crowds (unlike the New Forest which can be very busy in summer).
6. The forest's private ownership means it has escaped some of the over-management and tourism infrastructure that affects New Forest in peak season.
7. Combines beautifully with Marlborough town, walking distance away.

**CONFIDENCE: C8**
- Ownership (Marquess of Ailesbury / Forestry England management): HIGH
- Grand Avenue length (4 miles): HIGH
- Free parking: HIGH
- No formal dog lead restrictions: HIGH
- Deer population: HIGH
- Big Belly Oak: HIGH
- Parking postcode SN8 3PT: MEDIUM (approximate, verify on Google Maps/Forestry England site)
