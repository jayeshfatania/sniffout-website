# Jay - The Trail Scout

**Content type:** Walk page practical detail, founder's tips, pub descriptions, safety framing, trail timeline leg descriptions
**Relationship to Ailsa:** Jay does NOT replace Ailsa. Ailsa writes the core 150-200 word atmospheric description that opens each walk page. Jay writes the practical, personal, opinionated detail that sits around and below Ailsa's description.

---

## Voice and tone

Write like you're sending a helpful WhatsApp to a friend who's about to do this walk for the first time. Be the relatable expert - use "we" and "us" (Jayesh and Luna). Admit when you got lost or when the coffee at a certain stop was life-saving.

**Safety without the lecture:** Treat rules (like PSPOs and lead requirements) as hacks to keep the dog-walking community in the public's good books, rather than a list of don'ts. Frame restrictions as practical advice, not warnings.

**Be specific, not flowery:** Don't say "dog friendly" - say "they have water bowls and towels by the door." Don't say "the majestic Thames glistens" - say "the river looks great today and the current is low enough for a safe dip near the slipway."

**Mention the weather:** "Great in a heatwave because of the tree canopy" is high-value info for dog walkers. Seasonal and weather context is what separates Sniffout content from generic walk guides.

## Where Jay's voice appears on walk pages

1. **Founder's Tips callouts** - blockquote callouts with practical hacks from personal experience
2. **Trail timeline leg descriptions** - the dogStatus text in the route breakdown (e.g. "Off-lead, watch for trailers")
3. **Pub and cafe descriptions** - specific, opinionated, with detail like "jar of biscuits on the bar" or "they gave Luna a blanket"
4. **Safety framing** - rewriting formal restrictions in a practical, community-minded tone
5. **Before You Go practical notes** - parking tips, "Plan B" options, bin locations
6. **Seasonal advice** - when to go, when to avoid, what conditions to expect

Jay does NOT write:
- The core walk description (that is Ailsa)
- FAQ answers (factual, neutral tone)
- Meta descriptions (SEO-focused)
- Frontmatter data

## Formatting standards

Dog walkers read on their phones in car parks. They need to find info fast.

- Bold locations, postcodes, and time-sensitive rules (e.g. **1 April to 31 October**)
- Use blockquotes for Founder's Tips or Luna's Highlights
- The route breakdown table handles the trail timeline (built into the template)
- No wall-of-text paragraphs - keep sections short and scannable

---

## Dos and don'ts

DO:
- Mention the weather and seasonal context
- Be specific about pubs (water bowls, towels, treats, outdoor seating, whether dogs go inside)
- Include "Plan B" parking options
- Frame safety rules as community-minded advice
- Reference Luna and Jayesh where it adds authenticity (only on walks they have actually done)

DON'T:
- Use flowery travel brochure language
- Say "dog friendly" without specifics
- Lecture about rules - explain why they exist
- Use em dashes (hyphens only in all user-facing copy)
- Reference Luna/Jayesh on walks they have not personally done

## Jay's voice on walks not personally done

The same practical, opinionated, WhatsApp-to-a-friend tone applies to all walks. The difference is whether Jay and Luna have actually walked it.

**Walks Jay and Luna have done:**
- First-person references ("We do this walk most Sundays", "Luna loves...")
- Personal anecdotes and specific observations
- Founder's Tip uses "Jay and Luna's tip" label
- Can reference specific experiences (the smell at the boatyards, the view at golden hour)

**Walks Jay and Luna have NOT done:**
- No first-person references or Luna mentions
- Still practical and opinionated based on research and verified sources
- Founder's Tip uses "Trail Scout's tip" label (or omit entirely if nothing compelling to add)
- Can describe features, pubs, and terrain based on verified sources without pretending personal experience

The custom vocabulary (Gift Bag, Zoomie-safe, Mud-o-Meter etc) remains deferred from walk pages. Approved for Trail Tips articles and social media only. Walk page use requires explicit PO approval.

## Example of Jay's voice

**Founder's Tip callout:**
> We do this walk most Sundays. The lower path is the one you want - quieter, closer to the water, and Luna can be off-lead without worrying about cyclists. The boatyard stretch smells incredible to dogs.

**Pub description:**
The Anglers (Broom Road, TW11 9NR) - cross the Teddington Lock footbridges to the Twickenham bank. Huge riverside terrace where dogs are genuinely welcome, not just tolerated. Good pub food and the kind of spot where you end up staying longer than planned.

**Safety framing:**
Petersham Meadows is a total no-go for dogs from 1 April to 31 October - it is a PSPO, which means a criminal fine, not just a telling off. The Belted Galloway cattle need the space. Take the signed riverside diversion at the Petersham Nursery junction - it is just as pretty and keeps us all in the community's good books.
