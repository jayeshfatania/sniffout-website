## Shedding and fur management

**TT01 - The Rubber Glove Trick**
Put on a standard rubber dishwashing glove, dampen it
slightly, and run your hand over the fabric. The static
and friction pulls the hair into easy-to-grab clumps.
Works on sofas, clothes, car seats.
Affiliate: rubber dishwashing gloves (Amazon)

**TT02 - The Squeegee Method**
Use a window squeegee on your carpets or rugs. The
rubber blade pulls up deeply embedded hair that even
the most expensive vacuums miss.
Affiliate: window squeegee (Amazon)

**TT03 - The High-Velocity Blow Dry**
After a bath, use a hairdryer on a cool setting outside.
Doing this outside blows out the loose undercoat before
it ever reaches your carpet.
Affiliate: pet hairdryer (Amazon)

**TT04 - Salmon Oil for Shedding**
A squirt of salmon oil in their food can improve skin
elasticity and coat health, which reduces the volume
of dead hair they drop.
Affiliate: salmon oil supplement (Amazon)

## Hot weather and walking

**TT05 - Frozen Cucumber and Carrot Sticks**
Freeze cucumber and carrot sticks to use as treats in
hot weather. Cooling, hydrating, and safe. Also good
for teething dogs.
Note: pair with foods to avoid (grapes, raisins,
onions, xylitol).
Affiliate: none needed

**TT06 - The Wet Sock Cool-Down**
In extreme heat, cooling the paws and underbelly is
more effective than cooling the back. If your dog
seems overheated, soak a clean sock or rag in cold
water and wipe down their paw pads. The evaporation
helps drop their body temperature quickly.
Affiliate: none needed

**TT07 - The Cooling Pressure Point**
If your dog is panting heavily, apply a cold damp
towel to their chest and armpits rather than their
back. This cools the blood flowing to the heart more
efficiently.
Affiliate: cooling towel (Amazon)

**TT08 - The Kiddie Pool Bobbing**
Fill a shallow tray or kiddie pool with an inch of
water and toss in some frozen blueberries. It
encourages them to stand in the water (cooling their
pads) while they fish for snacks.
Affiliate: paddling pool (Amazon)

**TT09 - The 5-Second Pavement Test**
Press the back of your hand to the pavement for 5
seconds. If you cannot hold it there, it is too hot
for paw pads. Works on tarmac, sand, and artificial
grass - which heats up faster than most people realise.
Affiliate: none needed

**TT10 - Harness Not Collar in Heat**
In warm weather, attach the lead to a harness rather
than a collar. A collar pressing on the throat
restricts the airway and reduces a dog's ability to
cool down through panting.
Affiliate: harness (Amazon)

**TT11 - Cool Water Not Ice Water for Heatstroke**
If your dog is showing signs of heatstroke, use cool
water not ice water. Ice water causes shock and makes
the situation worse. Cool water on the underbelly and
paw pads while you call the vet.
Affiliate: none needed

**TT12 - Woodland in Summer, Open Walks in Winter**
Swap your routes seasonally. Woodland in summer for
shade, open ground in winter for warmth and dryness.
Most people do this instinctively but never think
about it deliberately.
Affiliate: none needed

## Water on walks

**TT13 - The Flip-Top Squeeze Bottle**
A squeezable water bottle where the top flips into a
bowl. I use one with Luna - her larger Shar Pei muzzle
(some nicknames given to Shar Peis are hippo face and
meat mouth!) means she needs more surface area to
drink from. Any excess water gets sucked back into the
bottle, which helps preserve it for later.
Affiliate: flip-top squeeze bottle (Amazon)

**TT14 - The Poo Bag Water Bowl**
If you forget your water bottle and really need water,
a poo bag works in a pinch. Roll the bag into a
smaller shape and add water. Not glamorous but it
works.
Affiliate: none needed

**TT15 - The Collapsible Bowl**
Collapsible water bowls store flat in a small shoulder
bag and can be used for water and food. Never leave
home without one.
Affiliate: collapsible bowl (Amazon)
