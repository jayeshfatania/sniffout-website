Teddington Lock marks the tidal limit of the Thames, the point where the river stops responding to the sea. From here south to Richmond Bridge the Thames Path follows one of its most varied stretches: boatyards giving way to the open flood plain of Petersham Meadows, then the Georgian terrace of Richmond riverside. The path is flat the whole way and the surface is good throughout the year, which makes it a genuinely reliable walk in any weather.

Petersham Meadows is a Site of Special Scientific Interest grazed by cattle from roughly April through to October or November. When cattle are present, dogs must be kept on leads in the meadows. Outside the grazing season the meadows are largely open to dogs. The Thames is accessible for paddling at multiple points along the south bank, and the river here is calm and safe for dogs to enter. Ham House National Trust grounds are a short detour across the river via the Teddington Lock footbridges; dogs are welcome in the grounds on leads.

The Dysart Petersham on Petersham Road is an upmarket pub with a bar area that takes dogs, set directly on the route through Petersham. In Richmond town centre, The White Hart on the Twickenham riverside is well-established as dog-welcoming. The practical note on parking: Teddington has pay-and-display options near the lock; Richmond town centre uses resident permit zones and public transport is the better option for the endpoint.

## Frequently asked questions

<div itemscope itemprop="mainEntity" itemtype="https://schema.org/Question">

### When are dogs required to be on leads at Petersham Meadows?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Leads are required in Petersham Meadows when cattle are present, which is typically from April or May through to October or November. The exact dates vary each year depending on the grazing management programme run by the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames. During grazing periods, the signage on the meadows makes the requirement clear. Outside the grazing season the meadows are largely open and dogs can walk freely, though the path remains a public right of way throughout the year.

### Can dogs swim in the Thames on this walk?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Yes. The Thames Path along the south bank between Teddington and Richmond has multiple informal bank access points where dogs can enter the water safely. The river is non-tidal from Teddington Lock upward and is generally calm at this stretch, with gently sloped banks at several points. The Teddington Lock weir area has a spot where dogs commonly access the water from the bank.

### Is there parking for this walk?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Teddington has pay-and-display car parks within a short walk of the lock, including options on Ferry Road (TW11 9NN). Richmond town centre is predominantly resident permit parking; arriving by public transport or train is the more practical option for the endpoint. If returning by train, Teddington Station to Richmond Station takes approximately 10 minutes on South Western Railway.

### What is there to see on the north bank near Teddington Lock?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Two footbridges at Teddington Lock cross to the Middlesex bank, giving access to Ham Lands, a Local Nature Reserve of rough grassland with no livestock where dogs can be off-lead throughout. Ham House National Trust property is a short walk south from the lock on the north bank; dogs are welcome in the grounds on leads. Marble Hill Park, managed by English Heritage, is further south toward Richmond and is open access throughout with dogs welcome off-lead in the parkland.
