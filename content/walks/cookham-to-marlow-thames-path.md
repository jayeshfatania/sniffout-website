From the National Trust car park at Cookham Moor, this Thames Path walk follows one of the most distinctive stretches of the middle river: open flood meadows, wooded Chiltern slopes on the far bank, and a pub you can only reach on foot. Cock Marsh is the first highlight, an unimproved wetland meadow purchased by local residents in 1934 and donated to the National Trust. Dogs are welcome off-lead across the marsh and the open meadow sections beyond it. Leads are required when cattle are present on grazing sections, typically spring through autumn. The river is accessible at multiple points throughout for dogs that want to wade in and cool down.

The Bounty pub sits on the south bank between Bourne End and Marlow with no road access whatsoever. Arriving on foot or by boat is the only option, which makes it feel genuinely remote for a Home Counties walk. Dogs are very welcome, water bowls are provided, and the outdoor riverside seating makes it a natural midpoint stop. The route continues through quieter meadow sections into Marlow, finishing at Higginson Park on the river. The Two Brewers on St Peter Street is the reliable dog-friendly endpoint in town. The simplest return is to walk back the same way; the Marlow branch line connects to Maidenhead for those with a car at Cookham. The Stanley Spencer Gallery in Cookham village is worth a look before or after the walk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can dogs go off-lead on Cock Marsh?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Yes. Cock Marsh is National Trust open access land and dogs are welcome off-lead throughout the marsh and the open meadow sections of this walk. The National Trust does not impose lead requirements on Cock Marsh in normal circumstances. Leads are required when cattle are present in the grazing sections between Cock Marsh and Marlow, which is typically from spring through autumn. Ground-nesting birds are present in the SSSI wetland sections in spring, so keep dogs close to the path between March and July.

### How do you get to The Bounty pub?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

The Bounty is only accessible by walking the Thames Path or arriving by boat. There is no road access. From Cookham Moor, it is approximately 5 km along the Thames Path, making it a natural midpoint stop. Dogs are very welcome at The Bounty, with outdoor riverside seating and water bowls provided.

### Is there parking at Cookham Moor?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Yes. The National Trust car park at Cookham Moor (SL6 9SG) is the standard start point. NT members park at no charge; non-members pay a small fee. The car park is adjacent to the Thames Path start. Higginson Park in Marlow (SL7 2AZ) has parking at the endpoint if you prefer to leave a second car there.

### How do I return to Cookham from Marlow?

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Walking back the same route is the simplest option. For a train return, Marlow Station connects to Maidenhead on the branch line, where you can pick up a service back toward Cookham. The journey is approximately 25-30 minutes in total. Verify current timetables at nationalrail.co.uk before visiting.
